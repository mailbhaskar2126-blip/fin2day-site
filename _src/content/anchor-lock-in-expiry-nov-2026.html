No new mainboard IPO opens for bidding this week, according to India IPO's daily tracker. But six recent listings face a quieter test. Between 8 and 12 November, the last of the shares bought by their anchor investors become free to sell.

Anchors are the big institutions, mostly mutual funds and foreign funds, that buy a slice of an IPO a day before it opens to everyone else. Across these six companies, anchors put in about ₹2,612 crore (FIN2DAY calculation, adding up the anchor books reported by IPO Guru).

What a lock-in is

Anchors get early, guaranteed allotment. In return, SEBI stops them from selling straight away. Until 2022, the lock-in was 30 days. After Zomato and Paytm shares fell 9% and 13% as their one-month anchor lock-ins ended, SEBI tightened the rule in December 2021, as reported by Business Standard.

  • Day 30Half of the anchor shares can be sold.
  • Day 90The other half is released. This applies to all issues that opened on or after 1 April 2022.

The idea is that anchors, whose names are used to build confidence in an IPO, should hold for longer than a month. Our IPO explainer covers where anchors fit in the process.

The six companies

All six went public in August 2026, so their 90-day clocks run out together. Since then, their fortunes have split sharply.

Latest share price versus IPO price. Source: IPO Guru, 9 November 2026.

Four trade well above their IPO price. Technocraft Ventures, issued at ₹212, was at ₹510.30 in IPO Guru's latest data. Milky Mist Dairy, issued at ₹140, has more than doubled. Dhoot Transmission, the biggest of the group with a ₹3,067 crore issue, is up about 84%.

Two are under water. LEAP India, which raised ₹2,480 crore at ₹159 a share, was at ₹121.97, down 23%. Ardee Industries was down about 13% from its ₹53 issue price.

How much stock is coming free

Because half of each anchor book was freed at day 30, roughly the other half unlocks now. At the IPO price, that's about ₹1,306 crore. At the latest prices, it's worth closer to ₹2,003 crore (FIN2DAY calculation).

Estimated value of anchor shares released at the 90-day mark. FIN2DAY calculation: half of each anchor book, revalued at IPO Guru's latest price. Assumes the 90-day half was unsold, as the rule requires.

Dhoot alone accounts for about ₹844 crore. Chittorgarh's data show its anchors were allotted 1,05,42,657 shares, so about 52.7 lakh shares come free (FIN2DAY calculation). Sources differ by a day on Dhoot's exact date: Chittorgarh lists 10 November, IPO Guru 11 November.

Do anchors actually sell?

A lock-in ending only means anchors can sell. In August 2026, two SEBI staff members published a study of 242 mainboard IPOs listed between April 2022 and October 2025. The authors note the views are their own, not SEBI's. It found anchors leave slowly.

Cumulative share of anchor holdings sold after listing, for 167 IPOs listed up to 2024. Source: SEBI staff study, via TaxGuru.

By day 90, anchors had sold only about 17% of their shares on average across all 242 IPOs. Even after a full year, about half was still held. The exception was small issues. For IPOs of up to ₹250 crore, anchors had sold 32.4% by day 90.

Who the anchor is matters too.

Share of anchor allotment sold by type of investor. FPIs: foreign portfolio investors. MFs: mutual funds. Source: SEBI staff study, via TaxGuru.

Foreign funds sell faster than mutual funds. That is worth checking for each stock: at Dhoot, mutual funds took 61% of the anchor book and foreign funds about 20%, according to Chittorgarh.

What happens to the price

The study found the clearest price effect at the first, 30-day unlock, and mainly when anchors sold heavily. Where they sold more than 10% of their shares, prices fell about 3.5% on average around the expiry. Where they sold less than 2.5%, the move was about −0.4%. The 90-day impact was "more muted".

In plain terms, an unlock is a supply event, not a verdict on the company. A stock trading far above its IPO price gives anchors a big profit to book. One trading below it may tempt some to cut losses, or to wait. Neither outcome is certain.
