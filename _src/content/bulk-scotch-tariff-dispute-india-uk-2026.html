When the India-UK trade agreement, known as CETA, came into force on 15 July 2026, Scotch whisky was its poster child. India's 150% import duty on Scotch was cut to 75% overnight, with a path down to 40% by the tenth year.

Four months later, the deal has hit its first big dispute. India's Directorate of Revenue Intelligence (DRI) says most of the Scotch India actually imports doesn't qualify for the cut at all.

What is bulk Scotch?

Most Scotch doesn't reach India in bottles. It arrives in large containers as bulk malt, grain or blended whisky. Indian companies then blend it with locally made spirit and bottle it here, as the brands sold as Indian-Made Foreign Liquor (IMFL). Pernod Ricard's Royal Stag and Blenders Pride and Diageo's McDowell's No.1 are made this way.

Scotch shipped to India in 2024, by type, in million litres of pure alcohol. Bulk was about 80% of the total. Source: Scotch Whisky Association data via The Whiskey Wash.

In 2024, India took 53.7 million litres of pure alcohol of Scotch, making it the biggest market by volume. Of that, 43.2 million litres, about four-fifths, was bulk. Reuters puts Scotch exports to India at $335 million in 2024.

The dispute

According to a DRI memo dated 4 November, reviewed by Reuters, bulk Scotch should be classified as a "compound alcoholic preparation", an input for making drinks, rather than finished whisky. The memo says the goods "are concentrates/compound preparations which are subjected to further manufacturing", and directs customs to charge the full 150%.

The argument turns on a tariff heading. Whisky sits under heading 2208, where the trade deal's cut applies. Preparations used to make beverages can fall under heading 2106, where the old rate still applies.

Basic customs duty on Scotch under different readings of the rules. Source: India-UK CETA schedule via India Briefing; DRI memo via Reuters.

Per Reuters, Pernod Ricard and Diageo each have more than 40 containers held at Indian ports, and Bacardi has five awaiting clearance. Investigators have searched company offices and questioned executives in recent weeks. No finding of wrongdoing has been announced.

Why the gap matters

Customs duty is charged on the import value, so doubling the rate has a big effect on cost.

Illustration: cost of $100 of bulk Scotch after basic customs duty only. State excise, other levies and margins come on top. FIN2DAY calculation.

At 75%, $100 of bulk whisky costs $175 once the duty is paid. At 150%, it costs $250, about 43% more. Companies have asked licensees and distributors for bank guarantees covering the extra 75% in case shipments are finally assessed at 150%, according to The Whiskey Wash.

The stakes are rising. Even before the pact, India's imports of UK spirits had roughly doubled in four years, from $192 million in FY22 to $408 million in FY26.

Who is saying what

  • Commerce ministryAt a meeting on 2 November, Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal reportedly told liquor companies the duty concession on bulk Scotch would not be impaired, and that the ministry would take the issue up with the finance ministry.
  • Revenue investigatorsThe DRI memo came two days later, which suggests the two ministries have not yet agreed. A senior finance ministry source told Reuters a resolution is being discussed.
  • Foreign spirits makersThe International Spirits and Wines Association of India, whose members include Diageo and Pernod Ricard, warned the classification risks undermining a key benefit of the deal.
  • Indian distillersThe Confederation of Indian Alcoholic Beverage Companies, whose members include Radico Khaitan and Allied Blenders, also objected. It argued that taxing bulk more than bottles would favour finished imports over blending in India.
  • UK governmentThe Department for Business and Trade said it is working with India to resolve the issue.

A familiar kind of trade fight

This is not a tariff war of the kind India has faced from the United States or Mexico. Nobody has raised a tariff. The question is how a product is classified, and that can matter as much as the headline rate. Trade deals list cuts by tariff line, so whichever heading customs picks decides which rate applies.

It also shows that a deal signed in Delhi and London still has to be applied at the port. The Scotch Whisky Association has estimated the pact could add $1.34 billion to its exports over five years, and much of that depends on bulk trade.

What to watch

Whether the finance ministry or CBIC issues a clarification on how bulk Scotch is classified, whether held containers are released at 75%, and whether the issue reaches the India-UK Subcommittee on Trade in Goods set up under the deal. Educational content only.
