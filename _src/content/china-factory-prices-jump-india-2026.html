On 9 November 2026, China's National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) reported that prices charged by Chinese factories, measured by the producer price index (PPI), were 5.7% higher in October than a year earlier. Prices of the raw materials those factories buy were up 7.9%.

That is a sharp turn. For more than three years, China's factory prices fell almost every month, as its suppliers competed hard on price. Now the world's biggest manufacturer is seeing its costs climb.

From 41 months of falls to 5.7% in seven months

China's producer price index (PPI), % change on a year earlier, selected months. August figure is a FIN2DAY calculation from the reported September change. Source: NBS via Continuum Economics, China Daily, Fibre2Fashion, CGTN.

China's PPI started falling in October 2022. In October 2025 it was still down 2.1% on a year earlier. In March 2026 it rose for the first time in 41 months, by 0.5%, according to China Daily. By September it was up 5.0%, the fastest since June 2022, Fibre2Fashion reported. October went higher again.

Prices also rose 0.7% in a single month in October. Across January to October, the PPI averaged 2.7% above last year.

What is pushing prices up

Mostly energy. The NBS said rising international energy prices drove October's increase. Oil and gas extraction prices rose 6% in the month and coal mining 2.7%. Six energy and chemical industries, from refined petroleum to gas supply, accounted for more than 80% of the monthly rise, as demand for coal and gas picked up ahead of winter.

Earlier in the year, officials also pointed to demand linked to artificial intelligence, computing power and electricity infrastructure. In September, the NBS said prices of computer, communications and electronic equipment rose 0.4% in the month.

Factories hot, shoppers cold

China's factory and consumer prices, October 2026, % change on a year earlier. Source: NBS via CGTN and Trading Economics.

Chinese households are not feeling the same heat. Consumer prices rose only 0.7%, the slowest since June. Food prices fell 2.9%, the seventh fall in a row, because of plentiful pork and weak spending, Trading Economics reported. Core inflation, which leaves out food and energy, slowed to 0.8%, its weakest in nine months.

That gap matters. When factory costs rise faster than prices at home, Chinese firms either absorb the squeeze or try to pass it on, including to buyers overseas.

Why India should notice

China is India's largest source of imports. Between April and August 2026, India bought $65.49 billion of goods from China, up from $51.56 billion a year earlier, commerce ministry data reported by Business Today show. Many of these imports are not finished goods but inputs that Indian factories turn into products, some of which India then exports.

How much of India's imports of selected inputs come from China. APIs are active pharmaceutical ingredients, 2025. Electronics categories are HS codes, 2025-26. Source: ThePrint, citing commerce ministry data and a Koan Advisory/Institute of Chinese Studies report.
  • MedicinesAbout 86% of India's imported antibiotic ingredients and about three-quarters of its top five API categories came from China in 2025, ThePrint reported. Drug makers have limited room to raise prices on many medicines, so higher input costs can squeeze margins.
  • Phones and electronicsChina supplied over half of India's imports of phone and network parts and nearly half of semiconductor devices in 2025-26. Imports of integrated circuits from China have risen sharply this year as India's smartphone exports grew.
  • BatteriesLithium-ion battery imports from China rose 85% in April–June 2026, to $1.43 billion.

How it could reach prices here

The link is not automatic. China's PPI measures prices at the factory gate inside China, not export prices, and Chinese firms facing weak demand at home may keep export prices low to win sales. Much of the rise is also in energy, which India already pays for directly. See what $100 oil does to India's import bill.

Still, for three years Chinese suppliers faced falling costs. If that has turned, it adds to cost pressure at a time when the rupee is weak and the RBI is already trying to cool inflation. See how the RBI is defending the rupee.

What to watch

China's November PPI, due in early December, and whether energy prices ease after the winter stocking season. In India, watch the wholesale price index for manufactured goods and electronics and pharma company margins in the October–December quarter. Educational content only, not investment advice.
