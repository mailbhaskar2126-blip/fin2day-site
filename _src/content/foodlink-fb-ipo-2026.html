A Mumbai caterer that feeds weddings, cricket events and corporate dinners is coming to the stock market. Foodlink F&B Holdings (India) Ltd opens its IPO on Thursday, 26 November, at a price band of ₹181 to ₹191 a share. Bidding closes on 30 November, anchor investors bid on 25 November, and the shares are expected to list on NSE and BSE on 3 December.

It is a small issue next to last week's ₹8,981 crore Mahanadi Coalfields sale. But it is a useful case study in reading an offer document: growing sales, a profit that doubled, and then a loss in the latest six months.

What Foodlink does

Foodlink calls itself a luxury food services company. It has three businesses:

  • Events cateringFood for weddings, corporate events and sports events. This is the biggest business and the most seasonal.
  • BanquetsRunning Jade Banquets and food services at convention centres.
  • RestaurantsCasual dining and cloud kitchens under brands such as China Bistro, India Bistro, Glocal and Art of Dum: 28 outlets as of the prospectus date, 15 in India and 13 abroad, mainly in the UAE.

It runs six centralised kitchens in India and one in Dubai, with capacity for more than 1 lakh meals a day, and has about 1,481 permanent employees. The chairman and managing director, Sanjay Manohar Vazirani, is the main promoter.

The offer: about half new money, half an exit

The issue is worth about ₹360.76 crore at the top of the band. It has two parts. A fresh issue of ₹192 crore creates new shares, and that money goes to the company. An offer for sale (OFS) of 88,35,382 shares, worth about ₹168.76 crore, lets existing holders sell. About 34 lakh of those shares come from the promoter group and about 54 lakh from investors. Of the fresh money, the largest single item is ₹50.81 crore for a UAE subsidiary to open four restaurants. Two new banquets, one in Rajasthan and one in Bengaluru, get ₹41.50 crore, and a new central kitchen in Rajasthan gets ₹21.40 crore. Only ₹21 crore goes to repaying debt, which is small: total borrowings were ₹22.38 crore at the end of September.

The minimum bid is one lot of 78 shares, about ₹14,898 at ₹191.

Sales are growing

Revenue from operations. The last bar covers only six months. Source: Foodlink RHP via Multibagg.

Revenue rose from ₹377 crore in FY24 to ₹567.50 crore in FY26, a gain of about 50% in two years. In the six months to September 2026, revenue was ₹219.83 crore. That is less than half of FY26, but catering is seasonal: the wedding season falls mostly in the second half of the financial year.

Profit is the harder question

Profit after tax. Losses shown in red. Source: Foodlink RHP via Multibagg and Alice Blue.

Foodlink moved from a loss of ₹15.35 crore in FY24 to a profit of ₹14.26 crore in FY25, then roughly doubled it to ₹29.11 crore in FY26. But in the first half of FY27 it reported a loss of ₹16.15 crore. The offer document's risk factors point to losses at its UAE subsidiary and to closures: four restaurants and two cloud kitchens shut in those six months.

The business mix is also shifting, away from events catering and towards restaurants and banquets.

What the price implies

The prospectus does not give a P/E, and there is no directly comparable listed company. Using the numbers it does give, here is a rough picture. All figures below are a FIN2DAY calculation at the ₹191 upper band.

FIN2DAY calculation

The fresh issue adds about 1.01 crore shares to the 5.23 crore already in issue, for about 6.24 crore shares after listing. That puts the market value at about ₹1,191 crore. On FY26 earnings per share of ₹5.58 (before the new shares), the price is about 34 times earnings. Spread FY26 profit over the larger post-issue share count and it is about 41 times. The latest six months were loss-making, so trailing earnings are lower still.

A price-to-earnings ratio is only a starting point; our P/E explainer covers what it can and can't tell you. Listed restaurant chains such as Jubilant FoodWorks, Devyani International and Westlife Foodworld are much larger and run franchise brands, so they are not a like-for-like comparison.
