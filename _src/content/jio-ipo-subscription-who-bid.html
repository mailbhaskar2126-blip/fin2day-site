Bidding for Jio Platforms' ₹30,213 crore IPO closed on Friday 23 October. The issue was subscribed 22.19 times. Investors bid for 438.26 crore shares against 19.75 crore on offer to the public, according to NSE data reported by Upstox. Including the anchor book, bids were worth more than ₹7 lakh crore, and there were nearly 1.4 crore applications.

Allotment was finalised on 27 October and the shares list on NSE and BSE on Wednesday 28 October at 10 am. Before then, here's what the bidding tells us. For the basics of the issue, see our explainer from opening day.

Who wanted it most

Final subscription by investor category. Source: NSE data via Upstox and HDFC Sky.

The headline 22 times hides a big gap between groups.

  • InstitutionsQualified institutional buyers (QIBs), such as mutual funds, insurers and foreign funds, bid for 345.76 crore shares against 4.90 crore set aside for them: 70.55 times. At ₹1,119, that is about ₹3.87 lakh crore of demand (FIN2DAY calculation).
  • Wealthy investorsNon-institutional investors (NIIs), who bid above ₹2 lakh, came in at 14 times.
  • Reliance shareholdersThe quota for existing RIL shareholders was booked 7.6 times.
  • RetailIndividuals bidding up to ₹2 lakh subscribed 2.72 times. Strong, but the calmest of the lot.

Most of it came on the last day

At the end of day two, the issue stood at about 5.6 times, with QIBs at 13.3 times and retail at 1.54 times, according to Deccan Chronicle. Comparing that with the final figures shows how much demand arrived in the last session.

Share of each category's final bids that came on the third day. FIN2DAY calculation; day-2 figures from Deccan Chronicle, final from NSE.

This is normal. Institutions often wait until the final hours, when they can see the full order book. Outlook Money reported that the issue was fully subscribed within 100 minutes of opening on 21 October, and that exchanges extended bidding hours to 9 am–7 pm to handle the volume.

How it compares with India's other giant IPOs

Overall and retail-portion subscription. LIC's retail figure is "two times" as reported. Sources: Business Standard, Angel One, Upstox.

The two previous record-holders drew far less interest. LIC in 2022 was subscribed 2.95 times. Hyundai Motor India in 2024 was subscribed 2.37 times, and its retail portion was only half filled. Jio is bigger than both and was booked roughly 7.5 to 9 times more heavily (FIN2DAY calculation).

What 2.72 times means for your allotment

Retail investors could bid in lots of 12 shares, costing ₹13,428 each at the top price. When the retail portion is oversubscribed, SEBI's rules require allotment by lottery, and each winner gets one lot of 12 shares, however many lots they applied for. Extra lots barely improve your chances.

Retail lots on offer vs lots bid for. FIN2DAY calculation from NSE share figures (8.50 crore shares offered, 23.12 crore bid).

About 70.9 lakh lots were available to retail investors, and about 1.93 crore lots were bid for. If every retail applicant had bid just one lot, roughly 1 in 2.7 would get shares. Because many applied for several lots, there are fewer applicants than lots bid for, so the real odds are better than that. The exact retail application count was not reported in the sources we checked.

Illustration

Say 1,000 people each bid 3 lots (36 shares). They have asked for 3,000 lots. If only 1,100 lots are available, every applicant can get one lot of 12 shares, and the leftover 100 lots are shared out under the allotment rules. Nobody gets the full 36 shares they asked for.

What oversubscription does and doesn't tell you

Heavy demand shows that large investors were keen at ₹1,119, a price that valued Jio at about ₹10.3 lakh crore. It does not fix the listing price. LIC's retail portion was oversubscribed too, and its shares opened below the issue price. Listing day prices depend on how many allottees sell, and on how many investors who missed out choose to buy on the market.

After the issue, Reliance holds about 64.5% of Jio Platforms, per the TradingView report.
