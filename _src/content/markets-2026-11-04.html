The Indian market made it five gains in a row on Wednesday, 4 November. The Nifty 50 rose 149.75 points, or 0.63%, to 23,960.60. The Sensex added 383.55 points, or 0.50%, to 77,301.40. HDFC Sky called it the longest winning streak for both indices in nearly five months.

The headline gain was modest, and Kotak Neo described a "relatively subdued", range-bound session. The real action was below the surface: smaller companies rose far more than the giants, and one sector, IT, went the other way.

Nifty 50 closing levels over the last seven sessions. Source: NSE via FIN2DAY's daily updates, HDFC Sky, Upstox.

Since its close of 22,698.85 on 28 October, the Nifty has gained 1,261.75 points, or about 5.6% in five sessions (FIN2DAY calculation). Yesterday's leg was led by consumer stocks (see Tuesday's update).

Smaller stocks did better

The Nifty Smallcap 100 jumped 2% to 19,026.40 and the Nifty Midcap 100 rose 1.55% to 59,505.50, according to Upstox. Bank Nifty gained 0.67% to 55,913.60, per Liquide. Across the exchange, 2,948 stocks advanced and 1,260 declined, HDFC Sky reported. Within the Nifty itself, 36 stocks rose and 14 fell.

How the main indices moved on 4 November. Source: Upstox, Liquide, NSE, BSE.

When the broader market beats the benchmark like this, it usually means buying is spread widely rather than concentrated in a few heavyweights. For how the index weights work, read how the Sensex and Nifty work.

Media, realty and metals up; IT down

Nifty Media was the best sector, up 5.15%. Realty rose 2.85% and Metal 2.80%, per Upstox. Energy, consumer durables, oil and gas, and FMCG all gained between 1.5% and 2.2%. Nifty IT was the only sectoral loser, down 1.50%.

  • Top losersTCS −2.52% (₹2,436), HCL Technologies −2.37%, Coal India −2.05%, BSE −1.75%, Infosys −1.73%.

The sources we checked did not give a specific reason for the IT fall. Before the open, Business Standard noted that a Democratic win in the US was being seen as negative for technology shares because of the prospect of tighter regulation. HDFC Sky also pointed to US restrictions on H-1B and student visas as a source of uncertainty for India.

Why the market rose

  • US midtermsDemocrats gained control of both houses of Congress, per Reuters as cited by Upstox. Vinod Nair of Geojit Investments said the result improved global sentiment, and that a more balanced US stance on trade and tariffs could draw more foreign money into emerging markets (PTI).
  • OilBrent crude for January delivery was at $102.55 a barrel around 3:35 pm, per Kotak Neo, down from about $105 on Tuesday. PTI put it at $102.84, down 0.73%. Hopes of an end to the US-Iran conflict are keeping prices lower, though analysts say uncertainty remains.
  • Foreign moneyForeign institutional investors bought a net ₹6,836.81 crore of shares on Tuesday, their third straight day of buying, while domestic institutions sold ₹3,678.85 crore (provisional data, via Upstox and PTI). See why FPIs and DIIs often pull in opposite directions.
  • EarningsLenskart's September-quarter profit rose 106% to ₹227 crore on revenue of ₹2,845 crore, and HUDCO's profit rose 28.3% to ₹911 crore, per APAC News Network.

The rupee rose 17 paise to close at 94.58 to the dollar (provisional), from 94.75 on Tuesday, PTI reported. The dollar index fell 0.53% to 101.18. On MCX, gold futures rose 2.33% and silver 3.95%, per Kotak Neo.

What to watch

  • US policy after the voteHDFC Sky said a Democratic Congress could affect Middle East tensions, energy prices, US fiscal policy and India-US trade talks.
  • Oil and the GulfLower crude has helped both shares and the rupee this week. Any flare-up could reverse that quickly.
  • ResultsBSE Ltd's September-quarter results are due. Its shares fell 1.77% to ₹3,600.90 ahead of the numbers, per Kotak Neo.
  • Diwali calendarMuhurat trading is on Sunday, 8 November, from 2:20 pm to 3:30 pm. Markets are shut on Tuesday, 10 November, for Diwali Balipratipada.
  • LevelsLiquide sees support for the Nifty near 23,800 and resistance near 24,100.
