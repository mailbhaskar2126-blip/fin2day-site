Wall Street had its worst day since April 2025 overnight, and Asian tech stocks crashed on Friday morning. Indian shares went the other way. On Friday, 6 November, the Sensex rose 1,718.01 points, or 2.17%, to 81,028.02. The Nifty 50 gained 493.75 points, or 2.01%, to 25,030.35, back above 25,000.

It was the seventh straight gain for both. HDFC Sky said that is the Nifty's longest winning run in 14 months and the Sensex's longest in 26 months. Both indices closed at their highest levels in more than eight months.

Nifty 50 closing levels, 28 October to 6 November 2026. Source: NSE via FIN2DAY's daily updates, HDFC Sky, Upstox.

From its 28 October close of 22,698.85, the Nifty has gained 2,331.50 points, or 10.3%, in seven sessions (FIN2DAY calculation). Over the week, the Sensex rose 8.8% and the Nifty 8.6%, per HDFC Sky, their best week since February 2021. Even so, both are still down for 2026: the Nifty by 4.2% and the Sensex by 4.9%.

A crash abroad, a rally at home

On Thursday night the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5%, the S&P 500 4.4% and the Dow 3.4%, per Business Standard and HDFC Sky. HDFC Sky linked the sell-off to weak Qualcomm results and worries about slowing spending on AI infrastructure. On Friday morning Korea's Kospi fell enough to trigger a circuit breaker, and Japan's Nikkei dropped as much as 8%, Business Standard reported.

GIFT Nifty futures pointed to a weak start, but the Nifty opened almost flat and climbed through the day. HDFC Sky pointed to money rotating away from AI-linked tech stocks, and to hopes that the US midterm result could hasten an end to the US–Iran conflict.

Foreign money in record size

The biggest support was foreign buying. Foreign institutional investors bought a net ₹14,974.68 crore of Indian shares on Thursday, per exchange data cited by PTI and Upstox. HDFC Sky called it their largest single-day purchase in nearly six years. Domestic institutions sold a net ₹10,444.54 crore, per Upstox. Big names up, most stocks down

The rally was narrow. The Nifty Midcap 100 slipped 0.02% to 60,606.65 and the Nifty Smallcap 100 fell 0.82% to 18,975.05, ending a four-day winning run, per Upstox and HDFC Sky. About 1,681 shares rose while 2,400 fell, HDFC Sky said.

The gains came from a few heavyweight stocks. Because the Sensex and Nifty weight companies by size, a strong day for big banks can lift the indices even when most shares fall.   • Dhanteras buyingTitan rose 3.42% on Dhanteras, the festival day when many Indians buy gold and jewellery. Kalyan Jewellers gained 5.49% in the midcap index, per Upstox.
  • Big falls in mid and small capsAstral fell 9.68% and Apar Industries 9.43% in the Midcap 100. TD Power Systems dropped 13.47% in the Smallcap 100, per Upstox.

Rupee gains 52 paise; oil firm

The rupee rose 52 paise to close at 93.89 to the dollar (provisional), PTI reported. It had settled at 94.41 on Thursday. Traders credited strong stocks and foreign inflows. Mirae Asset Sharekhan's Anuj Choudhary told PTI he expects a range of 93.65 to 94.20 for now.

Oil did not help as much as earlier this week. Brent crude January futures stood at $97.62 a barrel at 3:39 pm, per Kotak Neo, after fresh attacks on oil tankers in West Asia. Brent was quoted near $100 in the morning. For Thursday's oil-led rally, see yesterday's update.
