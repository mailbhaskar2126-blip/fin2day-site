After Wednesday's 773-point jump, Thursday was a day of pausing for breath. On 12 November, the Nifty 50 rose 44.30 points, or 0.18%, to 25,299.35. The Sensex added just 31.61 points, or 0.04%, to 81,911.01. It was the Nifty's third straight day of gains.

The headline numbers hide a busy session. The Nifty swung between 25,142.95 and 25,384.80, a range of about 240 points. Money moved out of autos, FMCG and IT and into banks, financials, property stocks and smaller companies.

Smaller stocks did better than the big names

Percentage change on the day. Source: Upstox market wrap, NSE, BSE.

The Nifty Smallcap 100 rose 1.12% to 19,706.75 and the Nifty Midcap 100 gained 0.85% to 62,039. Upstox said small caps beat the Nifty for a second straight session. Within the Nifty 50 itself, only 20 stocks rose and 30 fell. Fear eased too: the India VIX, a gauge of expected swings, fell 5.38% to 16.61.

Realty led, autos lagged

Nifty sectoral indices, percentage change on the day. Source: Upstox market wrap, Kotak Neo.

Property stocks had their best day in a while, with the Nifty Realty index up about 4.5%. Media gained 2.46% and PSU banks 1.51%. Upstox said bank results showing better asset quality and loan growth kept lenders in demand, and the Nifty Capital Market index hit a record high.

On the other side, the Nifty Auto index fell 1.02%, FMCG 0.74% and IT 0.41%. Business Standard and HDFC Sky described this as profit-taking after a sharp rally. Both indices are up nearly 10% so far in November.

Who moved the Nifty

Biggest Nifty 50 gainers and losers, percentage change. Source: Angel One, Upstox.

Insurers and lenders topped the list. HDFC Life rose 4.23% and Shriram Finance 3.58%. Bajaj Finance gained 3.36%; HDFC Sky linked the move to a share sale of more than $1 billion to institutional investors. Consumer names fell: Nestle India lost 2.10% and Hindustan Unilever 1.47%, while M&M slipped 1.90% a day after leading the rally.

Results drove big moves outside the Nifty. Page Industries fell about 13% after its September-quarter numbers, and HAL dropped about 4% after its results. Godrej Industries jumped after Temasek agreed to invest ₹2,500 crore in Godrej Capital.

Why the market was choppy

  • Foreign buyingForeign investors bought a net ₹12,563.33 crore of Indian shares on Wednesday, while domestic institutions sold ₹8,434.53 crore, according to exchange data cited by Upstox. HDFC Sky said November is on course for the strongest month of foreign buying since September 2024.
  • Cheaper oilBrent crude fell about 1% in the morning to $91.72 a barrel on hopes of progress in Russia-Ukraine and Iran talks, and a build in US crude stocks.
  • US yields and AsiaHot US inflation data kept the 10-year Treasury yield near 4.99%, HDFC Sky said. Asian markets fell, with the Hang Seng down 1.46% and the Nikkei down 0.99%.

The rupee weakened

The rupee fell 33 paise to close at 94.04 to the dollar, according to PTI, from 93.71 on Wednesday. Dealers pointed to a stronger dollar as markets priced in a possible US rate hike in December. The dollar index stood at 103.03 and Brent futures at $92.25 a barrel. Our earlier story explains how the RBI is defending the rupee.

Also on Thursday: inflation crosses 6%

India CPI inflation, year on year. Source: MoSPI via ChiniMandi and IndexBox. October figures are provisional.

Data released on Thursday showed India's retail inflation rose to 6.09% in October from 5.54% in September, above the 6% upper limit of the RBI's target band. Food inflation climbed to 8.94%, with onion prices more than double a year ago. September's spike is covered in our earlier explainer.

What to watch next

  • Inflation reactionFriday is the market's first chance to react to the 6.09% print. The RBI raised the repo rate to 5.50% in October; its next policy meeting is due on 2–4 December.
  • LevelsAn LKP Securities analyst quoted by Business Standard sees support at 25,000 and resistance at 25,500.
  • Results and flowsMore September-quarter results, foreign fund flows, US bond yields, crude oil and the rupee remain the main drivers.
