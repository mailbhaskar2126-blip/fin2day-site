After a long climb, the market took a breather. On 18 November, the Nifty 50 fell 159.45 points, or 0.63%, to 25,227.95. The Sensex lost 500.38 points, or 0.61%, to 82,031.81. It was the first fall in three sessions, after Monday's and Tuesday's gains in our 17 November update.

The Nifty opened at 25,323.15 and touched 25,390.80, just above Tuesday's close, before sliding to a low of 25,141.80, Angel One data show. Nearly ₹5 lakh crore was wiped off the value of BSE-listed companies during the day, HDFC Sky reported.

Why the market fell: a breather after a sharp run

Nifty 50 closing level, last seven trading sessions (markets were shut on 10 November). Source: NSE, via FIN2DAY daily updates, Angel One, HDFC Sky.

The main reason was profit-taking. According to HDFC Sky, the Nifty had rallied about 13% in just 12 sessions, and the Nifty and Sensex were up about 9.4% and 10.2% in November through Tuesday. Analysts said that left the market in "overbought" territory, where a pullback is common. Kotak Neo summed it up: "Bears finally made their way in after days of sustained rally."

Global cues did not help. On Tuesday night, the Dow Jones fell 1.64% and the S&P 500 1.03%. The US 10-year bond yield was near 5.10%, and markets are weighing a possible quarter-point Fed rate hike in December. Tensions between the US and Iran also stayed high, HDFC Sky said.

The selling was broad. On the BSE, 2,800 shares fell and 1,448 rose. Inside the Nifty 50, only 15 stocks gained. Even so, the market's fear gauge barely moved: India VIX closed at 15.56, up 0.26%. New to the indices? Read how the Sensex and Nifty work.

Consumer durables and metals led the fall

Nifty sectoral and broader indices, percentage change on the day. Consumer durables, financial services and PSU banks are approximate. Source: Kotak Neo, HDFC Sky market wraps.

HDFC Sky counted 13 of 16 major sectoral indices in the red. Consumer durables fell about 3%, dragged by jewellers and appliance makers. The Nifty Metal index lost 2.46%: a stronger US dollar and rising expectations of US rate hikes weighed on global metal prices. IT fell 1.24% and auto stocks more than 1%. Smaller companies were hit harder than the Nifty: the Nifty Midcap 100 fell 1.22%.

Top movers

Biggest Nifty 50 gainers and losers, percentage change on the day. Source: Angel One, HDFC Sky (NSE prices).

Titan was the worst Nifty stock, down 5.30% to ₹4,648.10, as jewellery stocks fell across the board. HDFC Life lost 3.29% and Hindalco 2.99%.

A few names bucked the trend. State Bank of India rose 2.38% to ₹1,135, and the PSU bank index ended about 0.4% higher. Cipla gained 2.09% after the US FDA closed an inspection of its InvaGen plant in New York with no action, HDFC Sky said.

Rupee, oil and foreign money

The rupee edged up 7 paise to close at 93.44 to the dollar (provisional), PTI reported. It had been stronger in the morning but gave up most of the gain as shares fell and the dollar index rose 0.30% to 103.79. "The Indian rupee reversed morning gains on a decline in domestic markets from higher levels and a strong dollar," said Anuj Choudhary of Mirae Asset Sharekhan. For background, see how the RBI defends the rupee.

Brent crude slipped 1.3% to $91.90 a barrel, HDFC Sky reported, helped by higher US crude stocks.

Foreign investors were still buying. They bought a net ₹4,195.38 crore of Indian shares on Tuesday, exchange data show, taking November's inflows to about $10 billion. Read how foreign and domestic flows move the market.
