One day after a sharp fall, buyers came straight back. On 19 November, the Nifty 50 rose 286.35 points, or 1.14%, to 25,514.30. The Sensex jumped 989.94 points, or 1.21%, to 83,021.75. That more than wiped out Wednesday's loss, covered in our 18 November update.

The day did not start well. The Nifty opened lower at 25,186.80 and slipped to 25,104.80, Angel One data show. From there it climbed about 410 points to finish at the day's high, HDFC Sky said. Marketcalls called it the Nifty's highest close in about nine months. The combined value of BSE-listed companies touched a record ₹512 lakh crore, according to Kotak Neo.

The Fed spooked IT, not the whole market

Nifty 50 closing level, last seven trading sessions. Source: NSE, via FIN2DAY daily updates, HDFC Sky.

The worry overnight came from Washington. Minutes of the US Federal Reserve's 27–28 October meeting, released on Wednesday, showed officials increasingly concerned that inflation could stay high. Marketcalls noted that most officials expect more rate hikes, and some are open to a half-point move in December. That is why the market opened lower.

The fear stayed mostly inside IT. Indian software companies earn much of their revenue in the US, so tighter US policy and slower tech spending hurt them first. The Nifty IT index fell 1.10% and was the only one of 16 major sectoral indices to end lower, HDFC Sky said. For how US rates reach India, see how a Fed hike travels to Indian markets.

Foreign money kept arriving. Foreign investors bought a net ₹5,866.78 crore of Indian shares on Wednesday, exchange data show, even as the market fell that day. HDFC Sky put November's net foreign buying at a record $11.28 billion. Read how foreign and domestic flows move the market.

Autos led a broad recovery

Nifty sectoral and broader indices, percentage change on the day. Source: Kotak Neo, Marketcalls (private banks, metal, pharma, realty).

The Nifty Auto index rose 4.40%. Analysts gave two reasons. Kotak Neo and Marketcalls pointed to softer raw material prices, as copper and aluminium fell, which eases costs for carmakers. HDFC Sky also cited resilient festive-season vehicle demand and lower crude prices.

Lower copper prices also lifted makers of air conditioners, and the consumer durables index gained 3.64%. FMCG rose 2.09% on hopes of relief on input costs. Banks extended their gains: PSU banks rose 2.29%, and the Bank Nifty closed above 63,000 for the first time, at 63,063, according to Marketcalls. The Nifty Midcap 100 gained 1.05% and the Smallcap 100 0.71%.

Top movers

Biggest Nifty 50 gainers and losers, percentage change on the day. Source: HDFC Sky (NSE prices).

Maruti Suzuki was the star, up 7.58% to ₹13,589. Marketcalls said it was the stock's best day in about 15 months. Eicher Motors gained 4.66% and Mahindra & Mahindra 4.46%. Inside the Nifty 50, 42 stocks rose and only 8 fell.

The losers were mostly IT names. Tech Mahindra fell 1.64%, Infosys 1.52% and TCS 1.34%. Apollo Hospitals was the only non-IT stock among the five biggest fallers, down 0.94%.

"A decisive move above 25,500 could possibly open the next upside target of around 25,900-26,000 in the near term," said Nagaraj Shetti of HDFC Securities. He placed immediate support at 25,200.

Rupee and oil

The rupee rose for a fourth straight session, closing 5 paise higher at 93.36 to the dollar (provisional), PTI reported. Wednesday's final close was 93.41. Foreign inflows helped, while a hawkish Fed and rising crude capped gains, said Anuj Choudhary of Mirae Asset Sharekhan. The dollar index eased 0.17% to 103.61.

Brent crude traded at $92.44 a barrel in futures, up 0.53%, according to PTI. HDFC Sky noted Brent is still down about 21.6% so far in November, which helps India's import bill.
