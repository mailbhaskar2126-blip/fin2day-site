Friday looked set for a bad start. Overnight, the Dow fell 2.19%, the S&P 500 2.02% and the Nasdaq 2.25% as investors dumped AI-linked shares. GIFT Nifty futures were down 218 points before the open, Business Standard reported.

Indian shares went the other way. The Nifty 50 closed 286.30 points, or 1.12%, higher at 25,800.60. The Sensex rose 897.96 points, or 1.08%, to 83,919.71. It was a second straight day of gains after Thursday's rally, covered in our 19 November update.

A rout everywhere else

Index moves, % change. Asian moves as reported by PTI during Friday's session; Nasdaq is Thursday's close. Source: PTI, NSE, BSE.

The selloff hit the markets most tied to the AI trade hardest. South Korea's Kospi fell 5.21% and Japan's Nikkei 5.06%, PTI reported. Taiwan dropped 4.98% in the morning, according to HDFC Sky.

The Nifty did dip at first. It opened at 25,368.00 and touched 25,345.20, Angel One data show. Then it climbed all day to finish near its high of 25,808.80. Analysts saw India as a relative winner. Ponmudi R of Enrich Money said the unwinding of crowded AI positions was a tailwind for Indian equities. Geojit's Vinod Nair said the cooling AI trade also helped Indian IT, which rose 1.30%.

Banks did the heavy lifting

Sector and broad-market index moves, % change. Source: BSE (via PTI), NSE (via Kotak Neo, Upstox).

The Nifty Bank index rose 2.49% and crossed 64,500 for the first time, Upstox reported. BSE's Bankex, PSU Bank and Top 10 Banks indices all hit record highs during the day. Kotak Mahindra Bank (+3.08%), HDFC Bank (+3.02%) and ICICI Bank (+2.22%) were among the top Sensex gainers.

The rally was narrow, though. The Nifty Midcap 100 gained only 0.66% and the Smallcap 100 slipped 0.13%. Of the 50 Nifty stocks, 32 rose and 18 fell.

Top five Nifty 50 gainers and losers, % change on the day. Source: Upstox, Angel One.

Hospital stocks were the day's standouts. Apollo Hospitals jumped 5.29% to a record high, and Max Healthcare rose 3.88%. Titan, Tata Steel and NTPC were the biggest drags.

Foreign money keeps coming

The main fuel is foreign buying. Foreign institutional investors bought a net ₹7,411.41 crore of Indian shares on Thursday, exchange data show. Their net purchases in the cash market this month reached about ₹1.02 lakh crore by 19 November, while domestic institutions were net sellers of about ₹56,927 crore, according to Tapetide's compilation. That is a sharp reversal from the pattern explained in our story on FPI selling and DII buying.

Those flows also lifted the rupee. It gained 65 paise to close at a provisional 92.67 to the dollar, from 93.32, PTI reported. That is the first time it has been stronger than 93 since April. Softer oil helped: Brent crude was down about 1.9% at around $90.24 a barrel during the day. The dollar index was at 103.87.

For the week, the Sensex gained 2.85% and the Nifty 2.61%. Since the start of November, the Nifty is up 11.91%, PTI calculated.
