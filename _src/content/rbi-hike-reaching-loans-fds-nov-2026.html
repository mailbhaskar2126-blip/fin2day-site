On 7 October 2026, the RBI raised the repo rate by 0.25 percentage points to 5.50%. Four weeks later, that single decision has reached Indian borrowers and savers at three very different speeds. Which one applies to you depends on the small print of your loan or deposit.

Fast lane: repo-linked loans

Most new floating-rate home and personal loans are tied to an external benchmark, usually the repo rate itself. Banks call it the RLLR or RBLR. When the repo moves, this rate moves by the same amount.

It happened the next morning. On 8 October, Bank of Baroda raised its repo-linked rate from 7.90% to 8.15%, Indian Bank from 7.95% to 8.20%, and Punjab National Bank from 8.10% to 8.35%. Bank of India and Indian Overseas Bank moved to 8.35% too. Your own loan rate is that benchmark plus a fixed spread, and it changes at your next reset date, which for these loans comes at least every three months.

Slow lane: MCLR loans

Older loans, and many business loans, are priced off a bank's own benchmark, the marginal cost of funds based lending rate (MCLR). It is built from what the bank pays for deposits and other funds, so it only rises as those costs rise.

That took almost four weeks. From 1 November, PNB raised its MCLR by 10 basis points across tenors, taking the one-year rate from 8.80% to 8.90%. Bank of India did the same for the one-year rate, from 8.80% to 8.90%, while leaving overnight and one-month rates unchanged. Indian Bank followed with a 10 basis point increase from 3 November.

MCLR loans usually reset once a year, so many borrowers won't see even this until their anniversary date. On a ₹30 lakh, 20-year loan, a 10 basis point rise, from 8.90% to 9.00%, adds about ₹193 to the monthly EMI (FIN2DAY calculation).

About two in three floating-rate loans now sit in the fast lane. That's why a hike reaches household EMIs much faster today than it did before 2019, when the external benchmark rule began. See how the repo rate reaches your EMI.

The FD lane: bank by bank

Deposit rates have no benchmark at all. Each bank decides, and the moves so far are selective. ICICI Bank and Indian Bank raised rates on selected fixed deposits, according to Lapaas Voice, but there has been no industry-wide increase. Bank of India launched a 500-day "Star Akshay" deposit at 6.85% for regular customers. At the start of November, the highest rate among public sector banks was 6.85% and DCB Bank offered up to 7.50%, according to a TradingView News report.

The starting point was low. In September, the month before the hike, the average rate on fresh term deposits fell to 5.61%, from 5.67% in August. The October figures, due at the end of November, will show the first real effect.

Why deposit rates are likely to follow

Banks are lending money faster than they are collecting it. In the year to 15 October, bank credit grew 17.94% to ₹232.54 lakh crore, while deposits grew 16.60% to ₹284.37 lakh crore. Credit has outpaced deposits every year since FY23, according to Business Standard.

New deposits and new loans in the year to 15 October 2026. FIN2DAY calculation from RBI fortnightly data.

For every ₹100 of new deposits, banks lent out about ₹87 in new loans, compared with about ₹82 across all their deposits (FIN2DAY calculation). Add the cash squeeze since September, which we covered in From ₹10 lakh crore to almost zero, and banks have a growing need to attract savers. Large foreign-currency (FCNR) deposit inflows earlier in the year had eased that pressure, which is one reason banks have been slow so far.

What it means for you

  • Repo-linked loanExpect the full 0.25 point at your next quarterly reset. Banks don't automatically extend your tenure; ask if you'd rather keep the EMI the same.
  • MCLR loanSmaller, later increases, timed to your annual reset. Check which benchmark your loan uses on your sanction letter or statement.
  • Fixed-rate loanUnaffected during the fixed period.
  • Fixed depositRates are rising unevenly, often through special tenures like 444 or 500 days. Existing FDs keep their old rate until maturity.
