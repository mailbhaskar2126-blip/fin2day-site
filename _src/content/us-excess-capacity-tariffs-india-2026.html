On 25 November 2026, the US Trade Representative, Jamieson Greer, proposed a new round of tariffs on 14 economies, including India. The charge this time is not Russian oil or forced labour. It is that these countries build more factory capacity than the world needs, and push the surplus into the US.

India's proposed rate is up to 25%. If India and the US sign their long-delayed trade agreement, it would be 17%.

What the US is proposing

The tariffs come from an eight-month investigation under Section 301 of the US Trade Act of 1974, the same law used against China in 2018. It looked at 16 economies. The USTR found that 14 of them follow policies that create or sustain "structural excess capacity" and hurt US commerce. It has not yet made a decision on the other two, Mexico and Norway.

India's 25% is the second highest on the list, behind only Vietnam's 35%. The richer economies that have already signed tariff deals with Washington, such as Japan and the EU, are capped at 15%.

The new duties also have a revenue purpose. In February 2026 the US Supreme Court struck down the broad tariffs imposed under an emergency-powers law. AP reports that this round is partly meant to replace that lost revenue, and could cover roughly three-fifths of US imports.

Why India is on the list

According to Business Standard, the USTR cited India's plans to expand manufacturing, inadequate labour protections, financial policies, barriers to market access, and failure to address global excess production. Business Today reports the USTR's findings name steel, solar, health and ICT goods, and policies such as Make in India, the production-linked incentive (PLI) schemes and Atmanirbhar Bharat. The Federal reports the USTR found India runs trade surpluses in pharmaceuticals, steel and ICT goods.

India's Commerce Ministry disagrees. In a statement it said India "engaged constructively throughout this investigation" and had told the US that India "does not have structural excess capacity in any sector." It said trade concerns should be settled in the bilateral trade agreement talks, and that it remains committed to concluding that deal early.

25% is not 25% on top

Three details make the headline number smaller than it sounds:

  • It is a ceilingThe proposal says "up to 25%", and it is "net of" India's existing Section 301 duty, the forced-labour tariff imposed in July. The old duty is counted inside the new ceiling, not added to it.
  • ExemptionsGoods already covered by US national-security (Section 232) tariffs are excluded for every country except China: steel, aluminium and copper, vehicles and parts, wood products, patented medicines and semiconductors.
  • Little stackingGreer told CNBC the tariffs would be "not really additive", with a "top-up" of a few percentage points in most cases.

The real fight is over the gap between 25% and 17%. As an illustration, on a $10,000 shipment of Indian goods that gap is $800 in tariff, or eight percentage points of price (FIN2DAY calculation, ignoring normal duties and exemptions).

Proposed rates for India with a trade deal, and for its main Asian competitors in labour-intensive exports, %. Source: USTR proposal via Husch Blackwell and The Federal.

With a deal, India would pay a slightly lower rate than Bangladesh, Vietnam and the big Southeast Asian exporters it competes with in garments, footwear and electronics. Without one, it would pay more than all of them except Vietnam. That is the real leverage in this proposal.

What is at stake

Trade between the two countries has kept growing despite two years of tariff turmoil. In April to September 2026, India's goods exports to the US rose 11.1% from a year earlier, and imports from the US rose 26%.

India's goods trade with the US, April to September of each year, $ billion. Source: Commerce Ministry data via ChiniMandi.

India's goods surplus with the US narrowed to $18.48 billion in those six months, from $20.07 billion a year earlier, as India bought more American goods. The momentum continued into October: exports to the US rose 39.25% that month, according to the Commerce Ministry. Under the February 2026 framework, India also said it intends to buy $500 billion of American goods over five years.

What happens next

Timeline of the Section 301 excess-capacity proposal. Sources: Business Today, The Federal, AP.

Nothing changes at the border yet. The USTR will take written comments until 5 January 2027 and hold hearings from 25 January. AP expects the duties to start in February or March at the earliest. Meanwhile the US–China trade truce runs to January, and China, which faces up to 40% if that truce lapses, has called "overcapacity" a "false proposition".

Why it matters

For Indian exporters, the question is not whether the US tariff on India goes up, but whether India ends up cheaper or dearer than Vietnam and Bangladesh. That depends on a trade deal that is still being negotiated, nine months after the February framework.
