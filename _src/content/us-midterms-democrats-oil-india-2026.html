On 3 November 2026, Americans voted in their midterm elections, held halfway through a president's four-year term. The Democrats, the opposition party, won majorities in both the House of Representatives and the Senate, according to projections by AP and ABC News. Before the vote, Republicans held the House 218 to 214 and the Senate 53 to 47, counting two independents who vote with Democrats. Final seat counts were not yet published in the reports we checked. The new Congress takes over early next year.

Wall Street rallied. But the biggest move, and the one that matters most for India, was in oil.

The day after, in numbers

How stock indices moved on 4 November 2026, the day after the US midterms. Oil fell far more: see below. Source: CNBC via StockTitan, Business Standard.

The Dow jumped 1,569 points, or 3.03%, to 53,356, its best day since April 2025. Brent crude fell 8.7% to $94.62 a barrel, its lowest settlement since 31 August, and US WTI crude fell 8.3% to $88.12.

Why an election moved oil

Oil had been driven up this year by the US-Israeli war on Iran, which disrupted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. After the results, Vice President JD Vance blamed the war, "which has pushed up inflation", for his party's losses, and President Trump acknowledged it had played a role. Traders read that as a sign the war could be wound down sooner than expected. "It does seem like a deal is more likely than not," said an analyst at ICIS, quoted by Bloomberg.

Brent crude, US$ per barrel. The 3 November figure is a FIN2DAY calculation from the 4 November settlement and its 8.7% fall. Source: Business Standard, Bloomberg via Rigzone.

Oil had already been easing from its six-month high near $118 in late October, on hopes of a US-Iran deal. The election added to that slide.

How it reached India

India imports most of the crude it uses, so cheaper oil works like a tax cut for the whole economy. Read what $100 crude did to India's import bill. On Wednesday, the effects showed up across Indian markets.

The chain from the US vote to Indian markets on 4 November 2026. Source: Business Standard, Bloomberg via Rigzone, CNBC via StockTitan.
  • RupeeClosed at ₹94.65 to the dollar, from ₹94.75, its fourth straight gain. It is still down about 5% in 2026, and not far from May's record low of ₹96.96.
  • BondsThe 10-year government bond yield fell nearly 11 basis points, to about 7.26%. "The bond market is responding to the US mid-term elections which could limit President Trump's powers," a public-sector bank dealer told Business Standard, with markets expecting his stance on tariffs and Iran to moderate.
  • SharesThe Nifty rose 0.63% to 23,960.60, a two-month high. Foreign investors bought a net ₹7,284 crore of Indian shares, the most in a day since 28 October 2025. IT stocks fell 1.5%. See Wednesday's market wrap.

What a Democratic Congress can and can't change

Congress controls the US budget and new laws. Analysts point out that it has far less say over the things India cares about most. "A Democratic victory would not automatically unwind existing tariffs," CMC Markets noted before the vote; trade, foreign policy and executive action "would remain largely under White House control," according to BBH. So the US tariffs already in place on Indian goods are not undone by this result. See how US tariffs on India got here.

Nor did it shift expectations for the US Federal Reserve. Markets still put the odds of a Fed rate hike on 9 December at about 81%, according to the CME FedWatch tool cited by Schwab, barely changed by the election. That matters for foreign flows into India, as we explained in the Fed's 'hawkish hold'. One more date to watch: current US government funding expires on 11 December, and a funding dispute could weigh on market sentiment, analysts say.

What history says, with a caveat

Average S&P 500 return in the six months after US midterm elections, since 1970. Source: BlackRock data cited by CMC Markets.

US stocks have tended to do well after midterms, as election uncertainty fades. Since 1950, the S&P 500 has returned an average of 15.4% in the year after a midterm, according to Capital Group. When the president's party lost control of Congress, as now, the six-month average was lower, at 10.4%. Averages from a handful of elections say little about any single year.

What to watch

The real question for India is not who runs Congress but whether oil keeps falling. Watch for signs of a US-Iran deal, shipping through Hormuz, and the Fed on 9 December. Educational content only, not investment advice.
