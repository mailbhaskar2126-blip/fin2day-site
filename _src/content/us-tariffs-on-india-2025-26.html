For Indian exporters, the last 14 months have been a lesson in how fast trade rules can change. Here's every turn, and where things stand now.

Headline US tariff rate on most Indian goods. Some products were always exempt, and sector tariffs on steel, aluminium and auto parts run separately.

2025: from 10% to 50%

  • 2 Apr 2025The US announces a 26% tariff on India. Within a week the "reciprocal" part is paused and a 10% baseline applies.
  • 7 Aug 2025A 25% tariff on Indian goods takes effect.
  • 27 Aug 2025Another 25% is added as a penalty for India buying Russian oil. Total: 50% on goods including gems and jewellery, garments, footwear, furniture, shrimp and chemicals.

The hit was real. India's goods exports to the US were about $86.5 billion a year, and the Global Trade Research Initiative (GTRI) warned they could fall towards $50 billion. Surat's diamond units reported shutdowns, and the rupee fell past 89 to the dollar at the time.

Some of India's biggest exports were spared throughout: medicines, smartphones and petroleum products were exempt from the reciprocal tariffs.

2026: deal, court, and a new legal route

  • 2 Feb 2026An interim trade deal. The US cuts the tariff to 18% and drops the Russia-oil penalty. India commits to stop buying Russian crude, lower its own tariffs on US goods, and buy more American products.
  • 20 Feb 2026The US Supreme Court strikes down tariffs imposed under the emergency powers law (IEEPA) that the reciprocal tariffs relied on.
  • 24 Feb 2026The US replaces them with a temporary 10% global levy under Section 122 of the Trade Act, which can last at most 150 days. GTRI estimated India's average effective tariff at about 13.4%, counting sector duties.
  • Late Jul 2026As Section 122 expires, the US imposes a 10% Section 301 duty on 17 economies including India, following an investigation into forced-labour import rules. India's government estimates it covers about 55% of exports to the US.

Who's still hurting

Labour-intensive sectors: textiles and garments, gems and jewellery, leather, engineering goods and chemicals now pay normal US duties plus 10%. Steel and aluminium still face 50% under separate national-security tariffs, and certain auto components 25%. In February the US also put a 126% duty on Indian solar imports.

The next threat

On 18 September 2026, President Trump signed a sanctions law directing tariffs of up to 100% on the five biggest buyers of Russian oil and gas. India committed to stop buying Russian crude under the February deal; which five countries will be named is not yet known. Three weeks later, the US agreed to buy Russian diesel itself. Why it matters

Tariffs are paid by importers, but they decide whether an Indian product can compete on price. For workers in Surat, Tiruppur or Moradabad, each turn of this rollercoaster has meant more or fewer orders. The history suggests these fights rarely end cleanly; see the US–China trade war of 2018–19.
