This was the week Big Tech reported its results, and investors did not like what they saw. Alphabet, Amazon and Intel all showed how much cash the AI race is burning. On Friday, Wall Street had its worst day since June, and the Nasdaq ended the week down 2%.

Here is what happened, and why it matters for Indian markets on Monday.

The week in US stocks

  • S&P 5007,580.07, down 0.6% for the week and 1.8% on Friday. Its third losing week in a row.
  • Nasdaq Composite25,437.00, down 2% for the week and 2.9% on Friday.
  • Dow Jones51,770.31, down 0.9% for the week and 572 points (1.1%) on Friday.
  • Russell 20002,811.84 (small companies), up 1.3% for the week despite a 1.7% fall on Friday.
Weekly change in the main US indexes, week to 30 October 2026. Source: AP.

Some perspective: the S&P 500 is still up 10.7% for 2026 and only 3.1% below its all-time high, according to the AP. Small companies have done best this year, up 13.3%.

Big Tech's cash problem

The trouble began on Thursday, when Alphabet fell more than 12%, its worst day on record, after weak search advertising and higher spending. On Friday it was Intel's turn. The chipmaker fell 28.6%, its biggest drop since 1974, after warning of weak PC demand and cash burn this year. Its revenue forecast for the December quarter, $15 billion to $16 billion, was below the $17.1 billion analysts expected.

Amazon's sales rose 12% to $201.9 billion and its cloud business AWS grew 39%. But it burned through $42.4 billion of free cash flow over the past 12 months, as it spent $94.8 billion on property and equipment, ABC News reported. Microsoft's cash flow was strong, but it raised $25 billion by selling debt, Reuters noted. "Cloud growth was strong, but the companies are spending huge amounts to deliver that growth," one analyst told Reuters.

Friday's moves in selected stocks and the Nasdaq Composite. Source: ABC News.

The sell-off spread to crypto. Bitcoin fell below $77,000, and crypto-linked stocks such as Coinbase and Strategy dropped sharply. This follows last week's scare over Meta's AI bill.

The Fed, bonds and inflation

On Wednesday, the Federal Reserve kept its key rate at 3.75% to 4% in a 10-2 vote. Two members, Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack, wanted a hike. New US inflation data then showed September PCE prices rose 0.5%, the biggest monthly rise since March. Markets now put about a 72% chance on a December hike, Trading Economics reported. Read our explainer on the Fed's 'hawkish hold'.

Even so, the 10-year Treasury yield ended Friday at about 4.93%, near a seven-week low, as investors moved from shares into safe government bonds. It fell about 36 basis points in October. See why bond prices and yields move in opposite directions.

Oil up, gold down

The December Brent contract settled at $117.64 a barrel on Friday, its expiry day, up 2.4%. That is about 0.8% above last Friday's settlement of $116.72 (FIN2DAY calculation). US crude (WTI) settled at $104.12. A tanker was struck by explosives in the Strait of Hormuz, and Houthi militants claimed strikes on Saudi energy sites. Brent rose 13.6% in October.

Gold went the other way. It fell 3.6% on Friday to about $3,876 an ounce, its lowest since October 2025, as investors priced in higher US rates for longer.

Why it matters for India

  • IT stocksThe Nifty IT index rose 2.3% on Friday, before the US tech sell-off. Weak US tech sentiment could test that on Monday. Indian IT companies earn much of their revenue from US clients.
  • Oil billBrent near $118 means a bigger import bill and more pressure on inflation. See what $100 oil does to India.
  • RupeeThe rupee gained 33 paise on Friday to close at 94.93 per dollar, helped by a softer dollar. Higher oil could pull it back.
  • Foreign moneyFPIs sold a net ₹943.60 crore on Thursday, while domestic institutions bought ₹1,924 crore. A December Fed hike would add to pressure on foreign flows. See how a Fed hike pulls money out of India.
  • Asian cuesOn Friday, South Korea's Kospi fell 3.5% and Japan's Nikkei 2.1% after the Bank of Japan held rates. Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 1.5%.

Indian shares start from a firmer base than a week ago. The Sensex closed Friday at 74,459.53 and the Nifty at 23,053.10, but two of every three stocks fell. Read our Friday market wrap.
