Europe shrugged off the AI scare this week, at least until Friday. Germany's DAX gained 2.5% and touched its highest level since early September. Asia had a rougher ride. Korean and Japanese shares swung wildly on chip news, and China's blue chips fell for a 12th week in a row.

Yesterday we covered the week on Wall Street, where weak results from Alphabet, Amazon and Intel hit tech shares. Here is the rest of the world, and what it means for India.

The week in Europe and Hong Kong

Weekly change in selected indexes. Sources: dpa-AFX (DAX), Trading Economics (STOXX 600), ad-hoc-news (Hang Seng), Corfe Capital (FTSE 100).
  • GermanyThe DAX rose 2.5% for the week and for October, closing Friday at 25,818.21. It gained 1.3% on Thursday, then fell 0.85% on Friday as oil climbed and US tech results disappointed, dpa-AFX reported.
  • EuropeThe STOXX Europe 600 rose 2.1% for the week to 646.75, even after slipping 0.36% on Friday. Tech shares led Friday's fall, with chip-equipment maker ASML down about 6%. The European Central Bank kept rates on hold.
  • LondonThe FTSE 100 edged up 0.37% to 10,729.78. Defence shares led the gains. The UK Budget raised the tax on banks by less than expected, and the bond market barely reacted.
  • Hong KongThe Hang Seng rose 1.46% on Friday to 23,294.42, ending the week up 0.87% and breaking a two-week losing streak. It still lost 5.36% in October. The Hang Seng Tech index stayed close to a two-year low.

Japan and Korea: a chip-driven rollercoaster

On Wednesday, South Korea's Kospi jumped 6.63% to close above 6,000, at 6,012.44, after SK hynix reported record quarterly revenue and profit. SK hynix shares rose 10.89% that day.

Two days later, the mood flipped. After Alphabet's slump on Wall Street, the Kospi fell 3.48% on Friday to 5,730.74. SK hynix lost 7.08% and Samsung Electronics 3.02%. Foreign investors sold a net 1.62 trillion won of Korean shares that day.

Japan's Nikkei 225 had risen for three days in a row before falling 2.12% on Friday to 63,290.11. The Bank of Japan kept its policy rate at 1.25% in an 8–1 vote, with one member wanting a hike. The Nikkei lost about 8% in October.

China: stocks slide, factories shrink

The CSI 300, which tracks China's 300 biggest listed companies, fell for a 12th straight week, the longest losing streak on record, Reuters reported. It has lost about 14% over those 12 weeks. The Shanghai Composite fell 1.44% on Friday to 3,599.02, its lowest close in more than a year. The tech-heavy ChiNext index dropped 4.1% that day as Chinese AI and chip stocks were sold.

State-backed investors were estimated to have bought as much as $25.5 billion of shares earlier in the week, according to a market report. It did not stop the slide.

The economy gave little comfort. China's official manufacturing purchasing managers' index (PMI) fell to 49.3 in October from 50.1 in September. A reading below 50 means activity is shrinking. The non-manufacturing PMI, which covers services and construction, fell to 49.7. Only high-tech manufacturing grew, at 51.3. The statistics bureau said some demand had been pulled forward before the National Day holiday.

Oil and gold

Oil rose to its highest level since April on worries about a possible new US strike on Iran, dpa-AFX reported. The expiring December Brent contract settled at $117.64 a barrel on Friday, while the January contract closed at $113.43. Gold fell about 3% over the week to roughly $3,885 an ounce.
