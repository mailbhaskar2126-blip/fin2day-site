US voters handed Democrats control of both houses of Congress this week. Two days later, Wall Street had its worst day in 19 months. Investors feared that a Democratic Congress could bring tougher rules for artificial intelligence and slow the building of AI data centres. The same election result sent oil sharply lower, on hopes that the US–Iran conflict could end sooner.

Here is what happened, and what it means for Indian markets on Monday.

  • Nasdaq Composite24,579.42, up 1.09% on Friday and down 3.4% for the week.
  • Dow Jones51,285.73, down 0.46% on Friday and 0.9% for the week.

Even after this week, the S&P 500 is still up about 7.8% for 2026, down from 10.7% a week ago, and the Nasdaq is up about 5.8% (FIN2DAY calculation from AP data).

Thursday's AI rout

The damage was done on Thursday. The S&P 500 fell 4.4% and the Nasdaq 100 6.3%, their worst day since the April 2025 tariff crash, TheStreet reported. The Nasdaq Composite fell more than 5.5% and entered correction territory, meaning a fall of 10% or more from its peak. Yahoo Finance said about $3 trillion of stock market value disappeared in a single session.

Selected moves on Thursday 5 November 2026. Source: Yahoo Finance, TheStreet.

Chipmakers were hit hardest. The PHLX Semiconductor Index dropped 12%, its worst day since March 2020, and Nvidia fell 9%. The worry, Reuters reported, is that Democrats won in some key data-centre states, which could slow the AI data-centre buildout. Candidates in both parties had campaigned against AI data centres, Yahoo Finance noted. If the AI building boom slows, the companies selling chips and power to it would earn less.

Friday brought a partial bounce. AMD rose about 10.5% after strong third-quarter results and Nvidia gained 3.3%. But for the week, US technology stocks fell 7%, Yahoo Finance reported. This follows last week's Big Tech cash-burn scare.

Strong jobs, and a likely Fed hike

On Friday, US data showed employers added 165,000 jobs in October, well above the 59,000 economists expected, per TheStreet. The unemployment rate was 4.1%. Good news for workers, but it makes it easier for the Federal Reserve to keep raising rates to fight inflation. Traders now see a 78% chance of a rate hike in December, up from 70%, Reuters reported.

The 10-year US Treasury yield ended near 4.86%. Gold slipped about 0.9% on Friday to roughly $4,075 an ounce. See why bond prices and yields move in opposite directions.

Oil's biggest fall in months

Oil moved the other way from fear. Brent crude ended the week near $98 a barrel, down about 12% over the week, according to Trading Economics and Trading and Investment News. Energy shares in the S&P 500 fell 11%.

How oil, energy shares and tech shares moved over the week to 6 November 2026. Sources: Trading Economics (Brent), Yahoo Finance (sectors), FIN2DAY calculation (S&P 500).

Traders read the Democrats' win as raising pressure on Washington to end the Iran conflict. Iran reportedly sent a proposal to Washington through Qatar, and is asking for an end to the US naval blockade, sanctions relief and the release of frozen assets, Trading Economics said. No deal has been signed, and tankers were attacked again in the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday. Mansfield Energy also pointed to signs that high prices are cutting global oil demand.

Why it matters for India

  • Oil billBrent near $98, after starting the week above $100 per Mansfield Energy, is the best news for India this week. Cheaper oil means a smaller import bill, less pressure on inflation and support for the rupee. See what $100 oil does to India.
  • IT and techThe global sell-off was about AI hardware and data centres. Indian IT services firms earn much of their revenue from US clients, so US tech sentiment can sway their shares.
  • Foreign moneyIndian shares rallied through the global rout this week. A December Fed hike, which makes US bonds more attractive, could test that. See how a Fed hike pulls money out of India and why FPIs and DIIs often move in opposite directions.
