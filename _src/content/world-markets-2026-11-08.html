Asia's chip-heavy markets had their worst week in years. Taiwan's main index lost 12.19% in five days, and Japan's Nikkei 225 fell nearly 6% on Friday alone. Hong Kong and mainland China held up far better. India, meanwhile, had one of its best weeks in a long time.

Yesterday we covered the week on Wall Street, where fears that a Democratic Congress will curb AI data centres hit chip stocks. Here is how that spread through Asia and Europe, and what it means for India.

Friday: a sell-off from Seoul to Frankfurt

How major markets moved on Friday 6 November. Sources: Seoul Economic Daily, Trading Economics, Focus Taiwan, ad-hoc-news, Capp Notes.
  • KoreaThe Kospi fell 6.02% to 5,059.69. Losses reached 8.55% soon after the open, triggering a 20-minute trading halt. SK hynix fell 10.44% and Samsung Electronics 6.35%.
  • JapanThe Nikkei 225 dropped 5.96% to 57,435, its fifth straight fall and its lowest level since April. Trading Economics said the weekly loss was more than 9%, the worst week since March 2020. The broader TOPIX fell 5.90%, with all 33 sectors down. SoftBank Group, Advantest and Tokyo Electron were among the biggest drags.
  • TaiwanThe Taiex fell 4.51% to 39,701.64, its fourth weekly loss in a row. TSMC briefly hit its daily limit down before closing 4.69% lower at NT$2,135. Foreign investors sold a net NT$91.19 billion of shares that day.
  • EuropeThe STOXX Europe 600 had been close to a record high on Thursday, then fell 1% on Friday to 648.33 as the tech sell-off spread. Germany's DAX lost 1.92% and London's FTSE 100 1.01%.

Korea: foreigners head for the exit

Foreign investors sold a net 2.769 trillion won (about $1.9 billion) of Korean shares on Friday, Seoul Economic Daily reported. Local institutions and retail investors bought most of what they sold.

Net buying and selling of Korean shares by investor type, 6 November. Source: Seoul Economic Daily.

The trigger was the same worry that hit Wall Street: that Democrats, who won both chambers of Congress in the US midterms, could restrict the building of AI data centres. Korea, Taiwan and Japan are home to many of the companies that make chips and equipment for those data centres. Some Korean brokers said the fears were overdone.

Hong Kong and China: the calmer corner

Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 1.27% on Friday to 24,481.86, after a 3.67% jump on Thursday. Even so, it gained more than 5% for the week, its best week since early March, according to Vibiz News. Tencent fell 2.6% and chipmaker SMIC 4.9% on Friday.

The Shanghai Composite slipped 1.10% on Friday to 3,649.18 but rose 1.39% for the week, ending a six-week losing streak. The Shenzhen Component gained 3.16%.

China's economy is still soft. The official factory survey (PMI) fell to 49.3 in October, below the 50 line that separates growth from contraction. On 2 November, the People's Bank of China made its overnight reverse repo rate the main policy rate, lending 541 billion yuan at 1.25%. Analysts at ANZ called it a targeted cut of 0.15 percentage points for banks. Citi called it a technical adjustment rather than a real rate cut.

Oil and gold

Brent crude settled at $98.32 a barrel on Friday. It lost more than 16% over the week, its steepest weekly fall since 2020, as traders bet the US election result opens a path to a US–Iran deal, CNBC reported. Gold rose 4.55% for the week to $4,060.20 an ounce at the New York close.

Why it matters for India

  • A rare splitWhile Asia's chip markets fell, the Nifty 50 rose 8.58% for the week to 25,030.35. India has few large chip and AI hardware companies, so the data-centre scare hurt it far less.
  • Foreign moneyForeign institutional investors bought about ₹45,960 crore of Indian shares over the five sessions, according to Liquide.
  • Cheaper oilIndia imports most of its crude. A Brent price below $100 eases pressure on the import bill, the rupee and fuel prices. See why $100 oil matters for India.
  • The riskStrong US jobs data has raised the odds of a December Fed rate hike, which has pulled money out of India before. See how a Fed hike hits India.
