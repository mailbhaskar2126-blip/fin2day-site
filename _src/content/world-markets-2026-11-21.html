Wall Street had its worst week in more than 18 months. The S&P 500 fell 8.6% in the week to Friday 20 November, and the Nasdaq Composite lost 10%, the Associated Press reported. A week earlier, the S&P 500 had posted its best week since early October. This week, a selloff in AI and chip stocks wiped out that gain and much more.

Here is what happened, and what it could mean for Indian markets on Monday.

The week in US stocks

  • S&P 5006,910.39 on Friday, down 3.7% on the day and 8.6% for the week. It is now in a correction, more than 10% below its 6 October record.
  • Nasdaq Composite22,617.73, down 5.3% on Friday and 10% for the week.
  • Dow Jones48,147.33, down 1.9% on Friday and 8% for the week.
Weekly change in the main US indexes, week to 20 November 2026. Source: AP.

Friday was the S&P 500's seventh straight losing day. The VIX, often called Wall Street's fear gauge, jumped 26.2% to 35.89, its highest close since late March, according to the Cappnotes market recap.

The damage leaves 2026 gains close to zero. The Nasdaq is now down for the year.

Change in the main US indexes in 2026 so far, as of 20 November. Source: AP.

Strong Nvidia results did not stop the slide

On Tuesday, 17 November, Nvidia reported quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier. Its data centre business brought in $107.6 billion. The company, now led by interim CEO Colette Kress, forecast about $130 billion for the current quarter. Profit, revenue and the forecast all beat analysts' estimates, AP reported.

The shares barely moved on the news. By Friday, Nvidia had crashed 14.01% in a single day to $125.53. Reuters reported that the US Securities and Exchange Commission is examining Nvidia's accounting, and that California's attorney general has sent a subpoena over whether its chips reached China, according to the Cappnotes recap. Other chipmakers fell almost as hard.

Biggest falls among large chip stocks on Friday 20 November 2026. Source: StockTitan.

Behind the selling is a broader doubt: whether the huge sums being spent on AI data centres will earn enough back. BNP Paribas's Bénédicte Lowe called it "panic selling"; Deutsche Bank's Binky Chadha said the market was "very oversold", StockTitan reported, citing CNBC.

Anthropic, the AI company that listed on Nasdaq this week at $83 a share, fell 8.5% on Friday, its second day of trading. It closed at $88.91, still about 7% above its IPO price.

The Fed still looks set to hike

Falling stocks have not changed the message from the US Federal Reserve. On Friday, Fed Governor Lisa Cook said that "waiting longer to implement rate increases risks getting behind the curve". Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson said she would be open to back-to-back hikes. Prediction market Kalshi put the chance of a quarter-point hike at the 9 December meeting at 90%.

Bonds rallied as investors looked for safety. The 10-year Treasury yield fell 12 basis points on Friday to 4.92%, its biggest drop since 22 October. It had been above 5% earlier in the week. The 2-year yield fell to 4.63%. The dollar index was about 103.84, just off Thursday's 19-month high.

Oil and gold

Brent crude settled at $92.57 a barrel on Friday, and US WTI crude at $83.98, which still left WTI down for a third straight week. Gold had a rough week too. Gold futures fell 4.63% on Friday alone to about $3,515 an ounce, a 14.5-month low, as investors sold to cover losses elsewhere and priced in a Fed hike. Why it matters for India

Indian shares have so far gone the other way. On Friday the Sensex closed at 83,919.71, even as Japan's Nikkei fell 4.1% and South Korea's Kospi 4.7% that morning, Reuters reported. Foreign investors bought a net ₹7,411.41 crore of Indian shares on Thursday alone, their 14th straight day of buying, per Marketcalls. Our 20 November markets update has the details.

  • Monday's openFriday's US selloff came after Indian markets had closed, so Indian stocks have not yet reacted to it. On Friday morning, GIFT Nifty pointed to a weaker start after a smaller US fall, but the Nifty still finished higher.
  • A shift in moneySome analysts see India as a winner when money leaves crowded AI trades in Taiwan, Korea and the US, because India has few AI-chip stocks. Whether that holds after a 10% Nasdaq week is the big test.
  • Indian ITA US slowdown would hurt IT firms' US revenue, though the cooling AI trade has so far helped their shares.
  • Rates and the rupeeA December Fed hike would make US bonds more attractive. As our Fed explainer shows, that has pulled foreign money out of India before. Lower oil helps India's import bill, explained in what $100 crude does to India.

What to watch next week

It is a short week in the US: markets are shut on Thursday for Thanksgiving and close early on Friday. Flash purchasing managers' surveys on Monday and speeches by Fed officials, including New York Fed President John Williams, will be watched for hints on rates.
