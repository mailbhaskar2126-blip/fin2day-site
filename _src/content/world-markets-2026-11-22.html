Asia's chip-heavy stock markets had one of their worst weeks in decades. Taiwan's Taiex fell 17.5% in the week to Friday 20 November, its worst week since 1991, Bloomberg reported. South Korea's Kospi lost 7.9% and Japan's Nikkei 225 7.7%. Reuters said a broad MSCI index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan was heading for its worst week since March 2020.

Yesterday we covered the week on Wall Street, where the Nasdaq fell 10% as AI and chip stocks crashed. Here is how Asia and Europe fared, what China did, and what it means for India.

The week: chip markets hit hardest

Weekly change in major Asian indexes, week to 20 November 2026. Shanghai and Hang Seng: Longbridge (etnet). Others: Trading and Investment News.

The markets that rose most on the AI boom fell furthest. Technology makes up more than 70% of the Taiex. On Friday alone it dropped 6.2% to 36,033.29. TSMC fell 4.8%, MediaTek almost 10%, and Delta Electronics hit its 10% daily limit. Taiwan's regulator brought in new curbs on short selling the same day.

In Seoul, the Kospi closed down 5.2% at 4,869.96, its lowest close since 16 January. Samsung Electronics fell about 7% and SK Hynix about 6%. Japan's Nikkei fell 5.1% to 56,436.65, a seven-month low, as SoftBank Group dropped 14.5%.

The trigger was the same as in New York: doubts over whether huge AI spending will pay off, Nvidia's slide after its results, new US export controls on advanced chips announced on 15 November, and bets that the US Federal Reserve will raise rates again in December.

China and Hong Kong: a smaller fall

Hong Kong's Hang Seng closed Friday at 22,410, down 1.9%, after touching its lowest level since late April 2025, The Standard reported. It lost 5.2% for the week. The Hang Seng Tech index fell 3.8% on Friday alone. Chipmaker Hua Hong plunged 10.2% after a Reuters report that TSMC is tightening restrictions on Chinese chip design firms. Alibaba bucked the trend, rising 3.5% after its cloud revenue grew 53%.

Mainland shares fell less. The Shanghai Composite ended at 3,533.79, down 1.23% on Friday and 2.77% for the week, its second straight weekly loss.

On policy, China kept its loan prime rates unchanged for an 18th month on Friday: 3.0% for one-year loans and 3.5% for five-year loans, which guide mortgage rates. October data showed industrial output and retail sales growth easing and home prices still falling. But the economy grew 4.5% in July–September, faster than expected, and the central bank recently made an overnight lending rate its main policy rate, which may have reduced the need for a cut. The offshore yuan slipped to a one-month low near 6.70 per dollar, Saxo noted. We looked at China's export surge in our trade surplus explainer.

Europe: worst losses in tech

Europe followed Asia lower. The Stoxx 600 fell 2% on Friday to 625.65, its fifth straight daily loss, and about 5.5% for the week on preliminary figures. Germany's DAX lost about 7% for the week and the FTSE 100 about 4%, dpa reported. European technology shares fell 5.39% on Friday alone. STMicroelectronics dropped nearly 11% and Infineon 7.6%.

Oil, gold and currencies

Brent crude settled at $92.57 a barrel on Friday, and US crude fell for a third straight week. Gold futures dropped 4.63% on Friday to about $3,515 an ounce, a 14.5-month low, as investors sold to raise cash. The dollar index was about 103.84, near a 19-month high. The yen was near 159 per dollar, close to the 160 level where traders fear Japan could step in, according to Saxo.

Why it matters for India

India went the other way. The Nifty 50 rose 2.61% for the week to 25,800.60 and the Sensex 2.85% to 83,919.71, their fourth straight weekly gain and highest closes in nine months, Goodreturns reported. The Bank Nifty jumped 5.90% to 64,635.20, close to its record high. Analysts credit renewed foreign buying and easier oil prices.

What to watch this week

Japan's markets are shut on Monday for a holiday. US flash purchasing managers' surveys come on Monday, and US markets close on Thursday for Thanksgiving, with an early close on Friday. Watch whether Taiwan's short-selling curbs and company buybacks steady Asian chip stocks, and whether foreign buying in India holds up.
