The Fed paused, but a December hike looks likely. What the 'hawkish hold' means for India

The US Federal Reserve kept rates at 3.75%–4% on 28 October, but two members wanted a hike and markets expect one in December. Here's why that matters for Indian yields, the rupee and the RBI.

On 28 October 2026, the US Federal Reserve kept its key interest rate unchanged at 3.75% to 4%. That came six weeks after it raised rates in September for the first time since July 2023.

A pause sounds like good news. Markets did not read it that way. The vote was 10–2, and both dissenters wanted a quarter-point hike straight away. The statement said inflation "remains elevated". Futures markets now put the odds of a December hike at about 85%, according to Bloomberg data cited by Admiral Markets. Traders call this a "hawkish hold": no move today, but the next move is probably up.

Why the Fed is split

US inflation rose to 3.7% in September, well above the Fed's 2% target. But look at what is driving it.

US consumer price inflation by category, September 2026. Energy is doing most of the damage.

Energy prices were up 20.4% from a year earlier, as oil stays above $100 a barrel. Core inflation, which strips out food and energy, was a much milder 2.6%. Hiring has also slowed: the US added just 29,000 jobs in September, and unemployment was 4.2%.

That is the dilemma. Hawks worry that costly fuel will seep into wages and other prices, so they want to act early. Others argue that interest rates can't pump more oil, and that hiking into a slowing job market risks a downturn. Chair Kevin Warsh described the debate inside the committee as "a heck of a family fight". The decision also came a week before the US midterm elections.

The number India should watch: the yield gap

Global investors compare what they can earn in different countries. The simplest yardstick is the 10-year government bond. India's 10-year yield closed at about 7.44% on 28 October, near its highest since March 2023. The US 10-year was trading near 5.04% after the Fed's decision.

The difference, India's premium over the US, is about 2.4 percentage points (FIN2DAY calculation). SBI Research put it at about 2.2 points in mid-October and noted that its 25-year average is about 4.1 points.

India's 10-year yield premium over US Treasuries. 28 Oct figure is a FIN2DAY calculation from closing and intraday yields; others from SBI Research.

Why does a smaller gap matter? Indian bonds carry extra risks for a foreign investor, chiefly that the rupee may fall and wipe out the return. When safe US bonds pay above 5%, the extra 2.4 points India offers looks thin. That reduces the pull for foreign money, and it pushes Indian yields higher as the market demands more. SBI Research has said India's 10-year yield could rise to 7.6%. For how bond prices and yields move, see our bonds explainer.

The squeeze on the RBI

The RBI raised its repo rate to 5.50% on 7 October, its first hike since 2023 (our explainer). The gap between the RBI's and the Fed's policy rates is now 1.5 percentage points. A December Fed hike would narrow it to 1.25 points (FIN2DAY calculation).

RBI and Fed policy rates. The last bar is a scenario, not a forecast.

That gap is not a hard rule, and the RBI sets rates mainly for Indian inflation. But a narrowing gap is one more reason the RBI may find it hard to cut rates soon, even if growth slows. Trading Economics notes that markets are already pricing in further RBI hikes over the next 12 months.

What about the rupee?

The rupee closed at ₹95.28 to the dollar on 28 October, down 21 paise on the day, ahead of the Fed decision. Kotak NEO's Anindya Banerjee said a "firmer-than-expected message on December could support the dollar and yields", which would weigh on emerging-market currencies. The dollar index itself barely moved after the announcement, at about 102. Read how the Fed's September hike pulled foreign money out of India.

What to watch

  • US dataOctober inflation and jobs reports, due before the Fed's next decision on 9 December.
  • OilEnergy is the main driver of US inflation. Cheaper crude would ease pressure on both the Fed and India.
  • The yield gapIf US 10-year yields stay above 5% while Indian yields hold near 7.4%, expect continued pressure on Indian bonds and the rupee.
