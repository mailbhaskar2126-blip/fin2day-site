Fed, ECB and Bank of Japan could all hike in 10 days. What a global rate wave means for India

After 32 rate cuts in 2025, the world's big central banks are raising rates again, many of them at the same time. December could bring three hikes in ten days. Here's how that ripples into the rupee, Indian bonds and the RBI.

In 2025, the world's major central banks cut interest rates 32 times, by a combined 8.5 percentage points. In 2026, the direction has flipped. And December could bring something rare: three of the world's biggest central banks raising rates within ten days.

  • 8–9 DecUS Federal Reserve. Futures markets put the odds of a 0.25-point hike at 97%, according to CME FedWatch data cited by TMGM on 25 November.
  • 17 DecEuropean Central Bank. Swap markets almost fully price a 0.25-point hike, taking its deposit rate to 2.75%.
  • 17–18 DecBank of Japan. In a Reuters poll, 61 of 71 economists expect a hike to 1.50%, which would make 2026 its first three-hike year in 37 years.

Where rates stand now

Key policy interest rates, late November 2026. US shows the top of the Fed's 3.75%–4% range. Source: ECB, Reuters, StockAnalysis, investingLive.

Rates are not the same everywhere, and they don't need to be. Each central bank sets its rate for its own economy. What matters for markets is the direction, and almost everywhere it is up.

Rate hikes announced in 2026 so far, by central bank. FIN2DAY count from central bank decisions reported by Reuters, ECB and others.

Australia's central bank has moved the most: five hikes this year, the latest on 3 November, taking its cash rate to 4.85%, an 18-year high. The ECB raised rates in June and September. The Bank of Japan did the same, reaching 1.25% in September, its highest in 31 years. The Fed hiked in September for the first time in three years, the Bank of England followed on 5 November, and India's RBI raised its repo rate to 5.5% on 7 October, its first hike in nearly four years.

Why everyone is hiking at once

The common thread is inflation that refuses to come down, pushed up in large part by energy. Reuters noted in October that the Fed and the Bank of Japan began raising rates after the US-Israeli war on Iran sent oil prices higher.

Inflation in three economies, September 2026. Most central banks aim for about 2%; Australia targets a 2–3% band. Source: StockAnalysis, investingLive, TMGM.

In the US, the minutes of the Fed's October meeting showed "nearly all" officials expected a December hike, and two floated a bigger half-point move. Fed Governor Waller said core inflation had been running at about 3.5% on an annualised basis over the previous three months. The Bank of England expects UK inflation to approach 5% by mid-2027. The ECB's own staff project eurozone inflation at 3.0% this year.

How it reaches India

Illustration: the main channel through which higher global rates affect Indian markets.

Foreign investors compare what they can earn in different countries. When safe government bonds in the US, Europe or Japan pay more, India's extra return over them shrinks, while India's extra risks, such as a falling rupee, stay the same. We explained this "yield gap" in our story on the Fed's October hold.

The Fed matters most, because the dollar is the world's main funding currency. But Japan is the one to watch this time. For years, investors borrowed cheaply in yen and put the money into higher-yielding assets around the world, a strategy called the "carry trade". When Japanese rates rise and the yen strengthens, some of those bets are unwound in a hurry, and money can leave emerging markets at the same time. The yen was still near 160 to the dollar in mid-November, despite Japan spending close to $100 billion to support it, according to the Reuters poll report.

The RBI's dilemma

The RBI hiked in October because Indian inflation was rising and the rupee was near record lows. Swap markets at the time priced about one percentage point of hikes over the following 12 months. In October, Bloomberg Economics expected two more 0.25-point RBI hikes, in December and February, which would take the repo rate to 6%.

A global hiking wave makes it harder for the RBI to stop early: if India stands still while others raise rates, the yield gap narrows further and the rupee can come under pressure again. See how the RBI has been defending the rupee. The cost is slower borrowing at home, as our look at loan and FD rates shows.

One thing working in India's favour

Oil has eased. Brent crude fell 4.27% to $87.50 a barrel on 24 November, according to the Rio Times market briefing. A cheaper oil bill reduces India's dollar needs and imported inflation, which takes some pressure off the rupee and the RBI, whatever other central banks do.

What to watch

The Fed's decision on 9 December comes first. Then the ECB and Bank of Japan on 17–18 December. A surprise from Japan, such as a faster-than-expected move, would matter more for global money flows than another widely expected Fed hike. Educational content only, not investment advice.
