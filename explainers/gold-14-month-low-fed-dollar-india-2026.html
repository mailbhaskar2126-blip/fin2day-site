Gold is 32% below its January record. Why rising US rates are pulling it down, and what it means for India

Spot gold slipped below $3,800 an ounce on 17 November, its lowest in 14 months, as markets bet on more Fed hikes. Indian prices have fallen too, but a weak rupee and a 15% import duty change the picture at home.

Gold set its all-time high in late January 2026. Since then it has lost about a third of its value. On the evening of 17 November, spot gold slipped below $3,800 an ounce for the first time since September 2025. Early on 18 November it was at $3,790, down 1.5% on the day, and one report had it briefly touching $3,660 before recovering.

That is about 32% below the record of $5,594.82 set on 29 January 2026. Gold is on track for its weakest year since 2015.

Why gold is falling

Gold pays no interest. When interest rates are low, that hardly matters. When safe assets pay well, holding gold has a real cost: the interest you give up.

That cost has jumped. The US Federal Reserve raised rates in September to 3.75%–4% and paused in October (our explainer). Traders now expect quarter-point hikes in December and January, and another by April. St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said the economy will likely need "several" more rate increases before spring 2027. The US 10-year Treasury yield has been above 5% on 27 days this year, according to Bloomberg, and was about 5.09% on 18 November.

Higher US rates also lift the dollar. The dollar index reached 103.65 overnight on 17–18 November, its highest since April 2025. Gold is priced in dollars, so a stronger dollar makes it dearer for everyone else, which cuts demand.

What it looks like in India

Indian buyers have felt the fall, but less sharply. On the morning of 18 November, MCX gold futures were at ₹1,31,616 per 10 grams, about 27% below the record of ₹1,80,779 touched on 29 January.

MCX gold futures, ₹ lakh per 10 grams, rounded. 18 November is the 9:20 am price. Source: Outlook Money.

Why the smaller fall? The rupee. It has weakened against the dollar this year (see how the RBI is defending it), so each dollar of gold costs more rupees. That cushions Indian prices on the way down, just as it amplified them on the way up.

How far gold has fallen from its 29 January 2026 record, in dollars and in rupees. FIN2DAY calculation from spot $5,594.82 to $3,790.18 and MCX ₹1,80,779 to ₹1,31,616. Source: 24/7 Wall St., Outlook Money.

The retail price has dropped fast in recent days. A 24-carat 10-gram piece cost ₹1,44,600 on 9 November and ₹1,33,260 on 18 November, a fall of ₹11,340, or 7.8% in nine days (FIN2DAY calculation).

Retail 24-carat gold price in India, ₹ per 10 grams, 9–18 November 2026. Axis in ₹ lakh. Indicative national rate. Source: Goodreturns.

India is importing far less gold

India buys almost all its gold from abroad, so the price shows up in the trade figures. In October, India imported $5.51 billion of gold, more than three times September's $1.70 billion but 63% less than the $14.72 billion of October 2025. The World Gold Council estimates October volumes at 70 tonnes, against 128 tonnes a year earlier.

India's monthly gold imports, US$ billion. Source: Department of Commerce via ChartForest and Outlook Money.

Prices are not the only reason. The government raised the import duty on gold from 6% to 15% in May 2026. From January to October, imports were down 6% by value and about 14% by volume, at 470 tonnes. The World Gold Council's Kavita Chacko said part of domestic demand "was met through alternative supply channels, including unofficial imports and recycled gold from old-gold exchange programmes". Retailers told the Council that old-gold exchanges made up 40–60% of festive sales at many stores.

Lower gold imports help the trade balance. India's merchandise trade deficit narrowed to $39.88 billion in October from $42.58 billion a year earlier, even as overall imports rose.

What it means for you

  • BuyersJewellery for the wedding season is cheaper than a few weeks ago. Even so, retailers told the World Gold Council that festive-season prices averaged about 10% more than last year.
  • HoldersGold bought near the January peak is showing a loss on paper. Gold has had long flat or falling stretches before, which is why many planners treat it as a small part of a portfolio, not the whole of it.
  • The economySmaller gold imports mean less demand for dollars, a modest help for the rupee and the current account.

What to watch

The Fed's next decision is on 9 December. A hike is widely expected; the bigger question is what it signals for 2027. Several big banks still forecast higher prices next year. Goldman Sachs cut its end-2026 target to $4,150 from $4,650 but expects central banks to keep buying about 60 tonnes a month. If US inflation cools and rate expectations ease, gold could rebound as quickly as it fell. For more on how rates and prices interact, see bond prices and yields, explained.
