After Jio, five IPOs list in one week. One was booked 241 times, another barely 5

Five mainboard companies raised about ₹2,581 crore and list between 2 and 5 November. The smallest, all-fresh issues drew the most demand; the biggest, a pure sale by Edelweiss, listed almost flat.

Jio Platforms listed on 28 October at ₹1,358, 21% above its ₹1,119 issue price. In its wake came a crowd of smaller issues. Between 2 and 5 November, 13 companies are due to list, according to Kotak Neo. Five of them are on the main board of NSE and BSE.

Together, those five raised about ₹2,581 crore (FIN2DAY calculation, adding up the issue sizes). But the demand for them could hardly have been more different.

The five issues

  • EAAA India AlternativesEdelweiss's alternatives arm: private credit, infrastructure and real-estate funds, with AUM of ₹95,345 crore. ₹1,200 crore at ₹1,665 a share. Listed 2 November.
  • SFC EnvironmentalWastewater treatment technology, with 728 installations commissioned. ₹667.53 crore at a band of ₹313 to ₹330. Lists today, 3 November.
  • M.K. Sons Fine JewelsGold and diamond jewellery, five showrooms in Mumbai and Ahmedabad. ₹275.40 crore at ₹153 to ₹162. Lists 4 November.
  • BLS PolymersPolymer compounds for cables and pipelines. ₹255 crore at ₹150. Lists today, 3 November.
  • Bombay Coated & Special SteelsCuts and processes steel coils for appliance and auto makers. ₹183.06 crore at ₹82. Lists 5 November.

From 5 times to 241 times

How many times each issue was subscribed overall. Bombay Coated figure as of 5:05 pm on the last day of bidding.

The smallest issues drew the biggest crowds. BLS Polymers received bids worth 241 times the shares on offer. Bombay Coated, the smallest of the five, was booked nearly 64 times. The largest, EAAA, was booked just over five times, and M.K. Sons a little under that.

Size explains part of this. A ₹183 crore issue needs far less money to fill many times over than a ₹1,200 crore one. But it isn't the whole story.

Who gets the money

An IPO can sell two kinds of shares. A fresh issue creates new shares, and the money goes to the company. An offer for sale (OFS) lets existing owners sell their shares, and the money goes to them. Our IPO explainer covers how this works.

Offer for sale as a share of each issue. FIN2DAY calculation from issue sizes.

EAAA was entirely an OFS: Edelweiss Securities and Investments sold ₹1,200 crore of shares, and the company itself received nothing. At SFC Environmental, ₹517.53 crore of the ₹667.53 crore went to the promoters. BLS Polymers and Bombay Coated, by contrast, were all fresh issue, raising money to expand a factory in Goa, repay loans and buy equipment.

Across the five, about ₹1,773 crore, or 69% of the money raised went to selling shareholders rather than into the companies (FIN2DAY calculation). That's not unusual, and an OFS isn't a bad sign by itself. Early investors and promoters have to exit somewhere. But it does change what the IPO is for.

Retail and institutions didn't always agree

Subscription of the retail quota, for individuals bidding up to ₹2 lakh.

At EAAA and SFC, institutions were far keener than individuals. Qualified institutional buyers, mostly mutual funds and insurers, bid for EAAA's shares 13.13 times, while the retail quota was barely filled at 1.03 times. SFC was similar: 18.81 times from institutions, 2.70 times from retail.

M.K. Sons ran the other way. Institutions bid just 1.84 times, while wealthier individuals in the non-institutional category bid 14.12 times. At BLS Polymers and Bombay Coated, every category was heavily oversubscribed.

Heavy oversubscription has a practical downside for small investors. When retail demand is 128 times the shares available, as at BLS Polymers, most applicants get no allotment at all. We saw the same pattern in who bid for Jio.

The first verdict

EAAA listed on NSE on 2 November at ₹1,675.50, a premium of just 0.63%. That works out to a gain of ₹94.50 on a nine-share lot. It rose to about ₹1,708 later in the session. Business Today put its market value at debut at about ₹10,881 crore. On last year's profit of ₹265.20 crore, that is about 41 times earnings (FIN2DAY calculation). See what a P/E ratio tells you.

Compare that with Jio's 21% first-day pop, and the lesson is that institutional demand alone doesn't guarantee a listing gain. Pricing matters just as much.

What to watch

BLS Polymers and SFC Environmental list today, M.K. Sons on 4 November and Bombay Coated on 5 November. The listing prices will show whether 241 times demand translates into a big first-day gain. Grey-market premiums are unofficial and often wrong. Educational content only, not investment advice.
