Mahanadi Coalfields IPO: Coal India sells ₹8,981 crore of its biggest coal arm, and MCL gets none of it

Coal India is selling 10% of its largest subsidiary at ₹129–₹136 a share. The company gets nothing; buyers get a big, profitable, high-dividend miner whose profits have been slipping.

A big public-sector IPO opens on Thursday. Mahanadi Coalfields Ltd (MCL), Coal India's largest coal-producing subsidiary, is selling shares worth ₹8,981 crore at a price band of ₹129 to ₹136. Bidding runs from 19 to 23 November, anchor investors bid on 18 November, and the shares are due to list on NSE and BSE on 27 November.

The key fact about this IPO: MCL itself will not receive a rupee.

A pure offer for sale

The whole issue is an offer for sale (OFS). Coal India is selling up to 66.18 crore existing shares, 10% of MCL. There is no fresh issue, so no new shares are created and the money goes to Coal India, which still keeps 90%. When a company raises fresh money, that money can go into new mines or paying off debt. In an OFS, buyers are simply taking over part of the owner's stake. Our explainer on how an IPO works covers the difference.

  • Price band₹129–₹136 a share. Minimum bid of 110 shares, about ₹14,960 at the top of the band.
  • Employees5% of the issue, with a ₹6 discount per share.
  • Coal India shareholders10% of the issue for individuals and HUFs who held Coal India shares on or before 13 November, the date the red herring prospectus was filed. Bids up to ₹2 lakh.
  • The restOf the net offer, up to 50% for institutions (QIBs), at least 35% for retail investors and at least 15% for non-institutional investors.
Who the shares are set aside for, in ₹ crore at the ₹136 upper band. Shares in each quota from the offer documents; values: FIN2DAY calculation. Source: Upstox, Chittorgarh.

Those quotas also explain a small puzzle. 66.18 crore shares at ₹136 comes to about ₹9,001 crore, not ₹8,981 crore. The gap of roughly ₹20 crore is the ₹6 employee discount on about 3.31 crore shares (FIN2DAY calculation).

What MCL is

MCL mines coal in Odisha. It produced 218.31 million tonnes in FY26, more than 28% of Coal India's output and about 22.4% of India's non-coking (power-plant) coal, according to a CRISIL report cited in the offer documents. It had 17 mines as of 30 June 2026, 14 of them opencast, and 20,473 permanent employees. Its audited reserves of 9,840 million tonnes would last about 45 years at the FY26 pace.

Mahanadi Coalfields net profit, FY24 to FY26. Output was 206.10, 225.17 and 218.31 million tonnes in the same years. Source: MCL offer documents via Chittorgarh, INDmoney.

The business is very profitable, but not growing fast. Net profit fell from ₹11,842 crore in FY24 to ₹10,698 crore in FY26, and output dipped in FY26 after rising the year before. In the June 2026 quarter MCL earned ₹2,398.78 crore.

The price tag

At ₹136, all 661.84 crore MCL shares are worth about ₹90,010 crore. FY26 earnings per share were ₹16.16, so the price is about 8.4 times last year's profit (both FIN2DAY calculations from offer-document figures). A Bloomberg report in early November had put MCL's possible value at up to $10 billion.

A single-digit price-to-earnings ratio is low by Indian market standards, but coal miners usually trade cheaply: their profits depend on government-set coal prices, demand from power plants and, over the long run, how fast India moves to cleaner power. A dividend machine

The main pitch is cash. DIPAM Secretary Arunish Chawla told CNBC-TV18 on 16 November that MCL "will continue to remain a high dividend-paying company". MCL paid ₹8,600 crore in dividends in FY24, ₹8,750 crore in FY25 and ₹8,400 crore in FY26, according to Moneycontrol.

Dividends paid as a share of net profit for the same year. Dividend figures from Moneycontrol, profit from offer documents; ratio: FIN2DAY calculation.

Government rules require profitable public-sector companies to pay out at least 30% of profit or 4% of net worth, whichever is higher. MCL has paid out far more. Two caveats: the prospectus says future dividends depend on cash flows and financial condition, and a ₹3,300 crore interim dividend declared after 1 October goes to Coal India, not to IPO buyers.

The BCCL precedent

MCL is the third Coal India subsidiary to list, after CMPDI and Bharat Coking Coal (BCCL). BCCL's ₹1,071 crore IPO in January was also a 10% OFS. It was subscribed 146.87 times and listed on 19 January at ₹45.21, 96.57% above its ₹23 issue price.

Issue size of the two Coal India subsidiary IPOs. Source: The Week, PSU Watch.

MCL is more than eight times bigger, which means far more shares to absorb. Grey-market trades on 16 November pointed to a premium of only ₹4.5 to ₹5.5 a share, or 3–4%, as reported by Investorgain. Grey-market prices are unofficial and often wrong. Like other recent IPOs, MCL's anchor investors will be locked in for a while; see what happens when that ends.

What to watch

Anchor demand on 18 November, the day-by-day subscription by category, and the allotment ahead of the 27 November listing. Coal India also plans to list South Eastern Coalfields this financial year. Educational content only, not investment advice.
