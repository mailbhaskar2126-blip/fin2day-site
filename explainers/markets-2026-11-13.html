Inflation and oil end the winning run: Nifty falls 155 points to 25,144 as realty, autos and metals slide

The first session after October's 6.09% inflation print ended with the Nifty down 0.61% and the Sensex at 81,592.45. Brent climbed back above $95 and the rupee slipped to 94.20, but foreign investors kept buying.

The market's three-day winning run ended on Friday. On 13 November, the Nifty 50 fell 155.05 points, or 0.61%, to 25,144.30. The Sensex lost 318.56 points, or 0.39%, to 81,592.45. At its weakest, the Nifty touched 25,054.40.

It was the first full session after data showed retail inflation at 6.09% in October, above the RBI's 6% upper limit. Add a jump in oil prices and high US bond yields, and buyers stepped back.

A broad, shallow fall

Percentage change on the day. Source: Upstox market wrap, NSE, BSE.

The selling was wide but not deep. Only 11 Nifty 50 stocks rose, 38 fell and one was unchanged. Across the NSE, 1,446 stocks advanced and 2,179 declined, according to Upstox. The Nifty Midcap 100 slipped 0.24% to 61,889.50 and the Nifty Smallcap 100 0.26% to 19,656.45, so smaller stocks held up better than the big names.

Nervousness actually eased: the India VIX, which tracks expected swings, fell 6.45% to 15.55. New to the indices? See how the Sensex and Nifty work.

Rate-sensitive sectors led the decline

Nifty sectoral indices, percentage change on the day. Source: Upstox market wrap.

Property stocks fell the most, with the Nifty Realty index down 1.39%. Autos, metals, private banks and IT all fell between 0.7% and 1.1%. HDFC Sky put the Nifty Bank index down 0.8%. Analysts cited by HDFC Sky said higher inflation could mean higher bond yields and tighter liquidity, which tends to hurt banks, realty, autos and infrastructure the most. See how bond yields work.

Who moved the Nifty

Biggest Nifty 50 gainers and losers, percentage change. Source: Upstox, HDFC Sky.

Shriram Finance fell 3.37% to ₹970, the worst in the index. Tata Motors PV dropped 3.31% after reporting a loss for the September quarter despite revenue rising 34%. Grasim lost 2.60% after posting a quarterly net loss of ₹234 crore. On the other side, Eicher Motors rose 1.34% and Asian Paints 0.53%.

Results drove big moves outside the index too. Business Standard reported Amber Enterprises up as much as 12.4% after a 60% jump in revenue, and FACT up as much as 5.32% after its profit rose 88%. Upstox said more than 660 companies reported results on Friday.

Why the market fell

  • InflationRetail inflation of 6.09% in October, up from 5.54% in September, has raised expectations of more RBI tightening. Geojit's Vinod Nair said it "reinforced expectations of a prolonged restrictive rate environment". The RBI's October move is explained in our rate-hike explainer.
  • OilBrent crude was trading at $95.47 a barrel in the afternoon, according to PTI. HDFC Sky said it was about $92 at Thursday's close. Upstox linked the jump to remarks by US President Trump that he would not hesitate to resume strikes on Iran.
  • US yieldsA forex adviser quoted by PTI put the US 10-year Treasury yield at 5.113% and saw an 85% chance of a Fed rate hike in December. Asian markets were weak too: Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 1.81%.

Foreign money is still coming in

One thing the fall did not reflect was foreign selling. Foreign investors bought a net ₹7,830.60 crore of Indian shares on Thursday, while domestic institutions sold ₹6,331.17 crore, according to exchange data cited by Upstox. HDFC Sky said it was the ninth straight day of foreign buying, and that November inflows have topped $8 billion, the most in a month since December 2020. That is a sharp turn from earlier this year, covered in our story on FPI selling.

The week in brief

Despite Friday's dip, HDFC Sky said 13 of 16 sectoral indices ended the week higher. The Smallcap index gained 3.6% for the week and the Midcap 2.1%. Nifty IT fell 3.2%, ending a six-week winning streak.

The rupee slipped

The rupee fell 8 paise to a provisional close of 94.20 to the dollar, PTI reported, from 94.12 on Thursday. The dollar index rose 0.12% to 103.16. Dealers blamed strong US inflation data, rising Treasury yields and hawkish comments from Fed officials. See how the RBI is defending the rupee.

What to watch next

  • LevelsSBI Securities' Sudeep Shah sees support at 24,900–24,850 for the Nifty and resistance at 25,424, the recent swing high.
  • RBI and the FedMarkets will watch for any signal on a further RBI hike after the inflation breach, and for US data that shifts the odds of a December Fed hike.
  • Oil and IranAny escalation in the Gulf would push oil, the rupee and inflation the wrong way.
  • CompaniesResults season is winding down. SpiceJet's board meets on Wednesday, 18 November, to consider raising fresh capital.
