Onions are up 108%. Why India's inflation just jumped to 5.54%

September's retail inflation is the highest since India's new CPI series began. Food did most of the damage, and villages are feeling it most.

India's retail inflation rose to 5.54% in September 2026, up from 4.83% in August, according to provisional Consumer Price Index (CPI) data released on 12 October. It's the highest reading since India moved to its new CPI series with 2024 as the base year.

The RBI's target is 4%, with a comfort band of 2% to 6%. At 5.54%, inflation is now uncomfortably close to the top of that band.

Food did most of the damage

Food inflation, measured by the Consumer Food Price Index, jumped to 7.74% from 5.97% a month earlier. Every major measure rose, but food rose the most.

Year-on-year CPI inflation, August vs September 2026 (provisional).

Onions, garlic and ginger led the spike

Onion prices were up 108.09% from a year earlier, more than double August's 48.78%. Ginger, garlic and lemon also jumped, while potato, tomato and coconut actually got cheaper.

Year-on-year price change by item, September 2026.

One caveat: onion has a small weight in the CPI basket, so a 108% rise in onions on its own moves the headline number only a little. It's the broad rise across food that pushed inflation up.

Villages are feeling it more than cities

  • Rural 6.08%Up from 5.24%. Already above the RBI's 6% upper limit. Food takes up a bigger share of rural household budgets, so food spikes hit harder.
  • Urban 4.88%Up from 4.31%. Still inside the comfort band, but rising.
  • StatesTamil Nadu had the highest inflation at 6.81%, followed by Odisha at 6.70%.

Why this matters for the RBI, and for you

This is the backdrop to the RBI's decision on 7 October to raise the repo rate from 5.25% to 5.50%, its first hike since 2023. It also raised its inflation forecast for this financial year to 5.2% from 5.0%. Read our explainer on the rate hike.

What it means for you

Floating-rate home and car loans linked to the repo rate can get costlier at the next reset, and new fixed deposits may pay a little more. If food prices stay high, another rate hike at the RBI's 2–4 December meeting is possible.

Dates to watch

  • 12 NovOctober inflation data. The first sign of whether onion and food prices are cooling.
  • 2–4 DecThe RBI's next monetary policy meeting: hike or pause.
