Onions are up 108%. Why India's inflation just jumped to 5.54%

September's retail inflation is the highest since India's new CPI series began. Food did most of the damage, and villages are feeling it most.

India's retail inflation rose to 5.54% in September 2026, up from 4.83% in August, according to provisional Consumer Price Index (CPI) data released on 12 October. It's the highest reading since India moved to its new CPI series with 2024 as the base year.

In plain terms: a basket of everyday goods and services that cost ₹100 in September 2025 cost about ₹105.54 in September 2026. A month earlier, the same basket was up only ₹4.83.

First, what CPI actually measures

The CPI tracks the prices of a fixed basket of things households buy: food, fuel, clothing, housing, health, education, transport and more. Prices are collected every month from markets across rural and urban India, and the Ministry of Statistics (MoSPI) publishes the result around the 12th of the following month.

Each item has a weight based on how much of a typical household budget it takes up. That's why a big jump in a small item moves the headline number less than a modest rise across a big category like food.

The RBI's job is to keep this number at 4%, with a comfort band of 2% to 6%. At 5.54%, inflation is now uncomfortably close to the top of that band.

Food did most of the damage

Food inflation, measured by the Consumer Food Price Index, jumped to 7.74% from 5.97% a month earlier. Every major measure rose in September, but food rose the most.

Year-on-year CPI inflation, August vs September 2026 (provisional).

Food matters so much because it's the single biggest part of the CPI basket. When food prices rise across the board, the headline number has nowhere to go but up.

Onions, garlic and ginger led the spike

Onion prices were up 108.09% from a year earlier, more than double August's 48.78%. Ginger (+62.85%), garlic (+54.62%) and lemon (+36.41%) also jumped. Not everything got dearer: potato (−11.50%), tomato (−8.62%) and coconut (−3.99%) were cheaper than a year ago.

Year-on-year price change by item, September 2026.

To put 108% in perspective: if onions cost ₹30 a kilo last September, a 108% rise means roughly ₹62 a kilo now. (Illustration only; actual prices vary by city.)

One caveat: onion has a small weight in the CPI basket, so a 108% rise in onions on its own moves the headline number only a little. It's the broad rise across food that pushed inflation up.

Why onion prices swing so wildly

Onions are one of India's most volatile staples. Most of the year's supply comes from a few harvests, and stored onions from the spring crop typically start running low around September and October, before the new kharif crop reaches markets. Any weather damage, delayed harvest or export change in that window can send prices sharply higher.

The good news is that this kind of spike is often seasonal. Once fresh supply arrives, prices usually ease, which is why the October and November numbers will matter.

Not just food: silver jewellery too

One of the biggest non-food movers was silver jewellery, up 87.80% from a year earlier. Jewellery is part of the CPI basket under personal goods, so when precious metal prices surge, it shows up in inflation too, even though most households don't buy it every month.

Villages are feeling it more than cities

  • Rural 6.08%Up from 5.24%. Already above the RBI's 6% upper limit. Food takes up a bigger share of rural household budgets, so food spikes hit harder.
  • Urban 4.88%Up from 4.31%. Still inside the comfort band, but rising.
  • StatesTamil Nadu had the highest inflation at 6.81%, followed by Odisha at 6.70%.

This gap matters. Rural families spend a larger share of what they earn on food, so the same rise in vegetable prices takes a bigger bite out of their monthly budget than it does for a typical city household.

Why this matters for the RBI, and for you

This is the backdrop to the RBI's decision on 7 October to raise the repo rate from 5.25% to 5.50%, its first hike since 2023. It also raised its inflation forecast for this financial year to 5.2% from 5.0%, and said it expects inflation to get close to 6% in October to December. Read our explainer on the rate hike.

Higher interest rates can't make onions grow faster. What they can do is cool overall demand and stop high food prices from spreading into everything else, like wages, rents and services. The RBI also watches oil and the rupee closely: crude above $100 a barrel and a weak rupee both make imports costlier and add to price pressure.

What it means for you

Floating-rate home and car loans linked to the repo rate can get costlier at the next reset, and new fixed deposits may pay a little more. Your kitchen budget is feeling it already. If food prices stay high, another rate hike at the RBI's 2–4 December meeting is possible.

Dates to watch

  • 12 NovOctober inflation data. The first sign of whether onion and food prices are cooling as the new crop arrives.
  • 2–4 DecThe RBI's next monetary policy meeting: hike or pause.

September figures are provisional and may be revised when MoSPI publishes final numbers.
