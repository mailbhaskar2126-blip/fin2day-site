S&P 500 has its best week since early October, up 2.5%, despite hot inflation and Nvidia's CEO quitting

The S&P 500 had its first winning week since early October. US inflation beat forecasts, the 10-year yield topped 5%, and Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive.

Wall Street had its first good week in more than a month. The S&P 500 rose 2.5% in the week to Friday 13 November, its first winning week since early October, the Associated Press reported. That came despite hotter-than-expected US inflation, a 10-year Treasury yield above 5%, and a shock at the world's most valuable chipmaker: Nvidia's chief executive, Jensen Huang, resigned on Thursday.

Here is what happened, and what it means for Indian markets on Monday.

The week in US stocks

  • S&P 5007,561.67 on Friday, down 0.4% on the day but up 2.5% for the week.
  • Nasdaq Composite25,136.27, down 0.9% on Friday and up 2.3% for the week.
  • Dow Jones52,347.55, up 0.1% on Friday and 2.1% for the week.
Weekly change in the main US indexes, week to 13 November 2026. Source: AP.

The rebound recovered most of last week's losses, when the S&P 500 fell 2.7% in an AI sell-off after the US midterm elections. For 2026 so far, AP data show the S&P 500 up 10.5%, the Nasdaq up 8.2% and the Dow up 8.9%.

Nvidia's CEO steps down

On Thursday, 12 November, Nvidia said Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and board member, effective immediately. In its filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, the company named finance chief Colette Kress interim CEO and Harvey C. Jones, a former Synopsys chief, as its first-ever board chair. The announcement gave no reason. Earlier in the week the board had opened an investigation after Huang admitted to an extramarital affair, Yahoo Finance reported.

Nvidia shares had already fallen 5.47% on Tuesday, after a drop of more than 4% on Monday, per Yahoo Finance. Tech stocks were the biggest drag on Friday, AP said. Nvidia still plans to report quarterly results on Tuesday, 17 November.

Inflation is still running hot

US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October from September, more than the 0.3% economists expected, CBS News reported. That is slower than September's 0.6% rise, but still fast. Core prices, which leave out food and energy, rose 0.3%. The US did not publish an annual inflation rate for October because of a data gap from the 2025 government shutdown.

Monthly change in US consumer prices. Source: CBS News, US Labor Department.

Wholesale prices rose 0.3% in October, a little below forecasts, Kitco reported. The annual wholesale rate climbed to 6.5%.

Market odds of a Fed rate hike at the 8–9 December meeting, after the October CPI report. Source: CBS News, CME FedWatch.

After the CPI report, futures markets put an 85% chance on a quarter-point Fed rate hike in December and a 15% chance of a half-point hike, according to CME data cited by CBS. The 10-year Treasury yield stood near 5.13% on Friday and the dollar index held above 103, Kitco said. Gold fell to its lowest level in more than a year before trading near $3,891 an ounce on Friday. See why bond prices and yields move in opposite directions.

Oil slips again

Brent crude was on course for a second straight weekly fall of about 2.4%, trading near $95.5 a barrel on Friday, HDFC Sky reported citing Reuters. President Trump said the US would keep its naval blockade of Iranian oil and that a deal with Tehran was not necessary, so traders now expect a long blockade rather than wider war. US crude stocks also rose by 4.7 million barrels, a sign of softer demand. Brent touched a ten-week low of $91.5 on 10 November, according to Trading Economics.

Why it matters for India

  • Monday's moodThe Nifty fell 0.61% to 25,144.30 on Friday as inflation worries ended its winning run. A firmer Wall Street week helps sentiment, but Friday's US tech losses could weigh. See our Friday market wrap.
  • Foreign moneyA US 10-year yield above 5% and a likely December Fed hike make US bonds more attractive to global funds. That can pull money out of Indian shares and weaken the rupee. See how a Fed hike pulls money out of India.
  • Oil billBrent in the mid-$90s, down from above $100 earlier this month, eases India's import bill and inflation pressure. See what $100 oil does to India.
  • IT and techIndian IT firms earn much of their revenue from US clients, so swings in US tech sentiment, including Nvidia's leadership change, can move their shares.

What to watch next week

  • Nvidia resultsDue Tuesday, 17 November, the first under an interim CEO. Its outlook will set the tone for AI and chip stocks worldwide.
  • Fed signalsAny comments from Fed officials ahead of the December meeting will move yields and the dollar.
  • Iran and oilMore tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz could push oil back up; signs of talks could push it lower.
