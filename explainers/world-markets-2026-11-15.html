Taiwan's Taiex posts its biggest weekly gain on record, up 10%, as China and Hong Kong slip before Trump meets Xi

Asia's chip markets bounced back after Nvidia's CEO quit. Japan rose 6.5% and Europe hit records, while China stayed cautious. Oil and gold both fell for the week.

Asia's chip markets bounced back hard this week. Taiwan's Taiex jumped 10.01%, its biggest weekly gain on record, a week after a 12% fall. Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 6.46%. Mainland China went the other way, and Hong Kong ended the week with three straight daily losses.

Yesterday we covered the week on Wall Street, where hot inflation and the resignation of Nvidia's chief executive, Jensen Huang, dominated. Here is how Asia and Europe reacted, and what it means for India.

The week: Taiwan and Japan recover

Weekly % change of major Asian indices, 9 to 13 November 2026. Source: Taiwan News, Trading Economics.

The Taiex closed Friday at 43,675.12, up 3,973 points for the week. That ended a four-week losing streak. According to Taiwan News, investors expect big cloud companies to spend more on custom AI chips, which would help local chip designers. On Friday, Global Unichip rose 9.54% and MediaTek 7.01%. TSMC, the largest company on the exchange, gained just 0.43%.

Japan's Nikkei closed at 61,142.35, its strongest week since June, helped by chip-equipment stocks. The broader Topix rose for a fifth day in a row. In Korea, the picture was weaker: the Kospi fell 0.98% on Friday to 5,290.53, as foreign investors sold chipmakers.

Friday: mixed after the Nvidia news

Daily % change on Friday 13 November 2026. Source: CappNotes, AJP, Trading Economics, QNA.

Huang resigned on Thursday after 33 years at Nvidia. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is interim CEO. Taiwan took the view that his exit does not change demand for AI chips. Hong Kong and China were more nervous. The Hang Seng fell 1.55% to 23,637.60, and the Hang Seng Tech index lost 3%.

China: cautious before Trump meets Xi

The Shanghai Composite slipped 0.59% on Friday to 3,634.35 and 0.41% for the week. The Shenzhen Component, heavier in technology, fell 2.05% on the day. Trading Economics said investors were cautious ahead of the meeting between Donald Trump and Xi Jinping. On 8 November Trump said he expected to meet Xi and Vladimir Putin at the APEC leaders' summit in China this week, Reuters reported.

The economic news was soft. Consumer prices rose just 0.7% from a year earlier in October, down from 0.9%. Trading Economics also reported that new yuan loans shrank in October, the first contraction for an October on record, and that the central bank has pledged stronger support for domestic demand.

Europe: best week since June

Europe's STOXX 600 hit record highs during the week and gained more than 2%, its strongest week since late June, according to QNA. It closed Friday flat at 664.14. Germany's DAX dipped 0.06% to 26,756.70, and London's FTSE 100 rose 0.36% to 10,902.56. Chip stocks such as ASML and Infineon fell on Friday, while insurers Allianz and Munich Re rose after results. Richemont slid about 7% after its sales growth slowed.

Oil, gold and the yen

Brent crude settlement price, US$ per barrel. Source: FinanceFeeds, Rigzone (Bloomberg).

Brent crude settled at $95.84 a barrel on Friday, its second weekly fall, down about 2.5% from $98.32 a week earlier (FIN2DAY calculation). It touched $91.49 on Tuesday, the lowest since August, before jumping 3.3% on Thursday on reports of attacks on Saudi oil sites and tankers near Hormuz. The IEA expects the oil market to be in surplus this quarter.

Gold ended near $3,879 an ounce, down nearly 4.5% for the week, as US bond yields rose and markets priced in a December Fed rate hike. In Tokyo, the yen weakened past 160 to the dollar for the first time in over two months, and Japan's finance minister warned that currency moves had become one-sided and rapid.

Why it matters for India

  • Foreign moneyLast week, Indian shares drew foreign buying as investors fled Asia's chip markets (our report). With Taiwan and Japan rebounding, some of that money may head back to North Asia.
  • OilBrent below $100 eases India's import bill and pressure on the rupee. But Thursday's 3.3% jump shows how quickly Gulf tension can reverse that. See what $100 oil does to India.
  • US ratesThe US 10-year yield was about 5.15% on Friday, and futures put the odds of a Fed hike on 9 December at about 90%. High US yields have been a big reason foreign investors sell Indian shares. See how a Fed hike pulls money out of India.
  • ChinaWeak Chinese demand and very low inflation keep China's export prices low. That helps Indian importers but adds competition for Indian manufacturers. See China's trade surplus and India.

What to watch this week

China publishes October industrial output and retail sales on Monday. The APEC summit and any Trump–Xi deal on trade could move Chinese and Hong Kong shares. In the US, October retail sales and Nvidia's quarterly results come on Tuesday, and minutes of the Fed's October meeting on Wednesday. Watch the yen too: another slide past 160 could bring intervention from Tokyo.
